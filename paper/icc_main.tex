% ── Abstract ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\begin{abstract}
Direct-to-Satellite (D2S) IoT terminals must pre-compensate large LEO Doppler
shifts before every LR-FHSS uplink from an on-board orbital prediction that is, in
practice, a \emph{stale} TLE propagated open-loop by SGP4. A natural idea is to
learn a residual correction on top of this physics baseline. On real Space-Track
TLE history for two BLACK~KITE LEO satellites (NORAD~66741, 68474), with strict
chronological leakage-free splits, a learned Doppler residual does \emph{not} beat
the open-loop baseline at any tested staleness (8--168~h), target-specific or
cross-satellite: the inter-TLE residual is near zero-mean and unpredictable. Rather
than discard learning, we gate it. The \emph{Evidence Gate} enables a learned
corrector only when a chronological held-out window beats the baseline by a
margin~$\gamma$, else falls back to open-loop compensation. On the real data the
gate \emph{closes}; in a controlled synthetic stress study it \emph{opens} under
dominant systematic drift, reducing simulated frequency-error, guard, and outage
proxies. All ``reference'' Doppler is model-derived (SGP4 from real TLE), not
measured; no live-satellite, link-layer, or hardware-radiated result is claimed.
\end{abstract}

% ── Introduction ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Direct-to-Satellite (D2S) IoT using Low-Earth Orbit (LEO) satellites enables
connectivity for power-constrained end-devices in remote areas, but the high
relative velocity (several~km/s in LEO) induces Doppler shifts of tens of
kHz---order $20$~kHz at 868~MHz and roughly $50$~kHz at S-band, depending on
carrier frequency and pass geometry---together with rapid Doppler-rate changes
that stress the physical layer~\cite{hurn2023lrfhss}. Long-Range
Frequency-Hopping Spread Spectrum (LR-FHSS), characterized in Semtech's
AN1200.64 note~\cite{semtech2023lrfhss}, achieves robust uplink by distributing
transmit energy across a grid of narrow frequency bins, providing frequency
diversity against Doppler uncertainty. Because the sub-channels are narrow,
residual frequency error after pre-compensation is costly: it widens the guard
the terminal must reserve, increases the chance of missing a hop bin, and spends
airtime and energy that a battery-powered, unattended node cannot recover.

The terminal must size these margins \emph{before} transmitting, using only the
orbital information it holds on board---in practice a \emph{stale} TLE propagated
open-loop by SGP4/SDP4~\cite{vallado2007fundamentals}. This is the deployable
physics baseline. A tempting hypothesis is that a learned model can predict the
stale-TLE$\rightarrow$truth Doppler residual and shrink the error. The central
question of this paper is therefore not how to recover a waveform once it
exists, but a transmitter-side decision: \emph{when is it justified to learn a
correction on top of the physics prediction, and when is the safe physics
default the right choice?}

We expose a gap in the naive ``always learn'' answer. On real TLE history for two
BLACK~KITE satellites, a learned residual corrector does not beat the open-loop
SGP4 / stale-TLE baseline at any tested staleness; the residual between
successive TLE solutions is near zero-mean and unpredictable, so an always-on
learner would, if anything, \emph{increase} the frequency error it is meant to
reduce. Our stance is to treat learning as an option that must be \emph{earned}
on held-out evidence, with the physics model as the safe default. We formalise
this as an \emph{Evidence Gate}: a learned corrector is deployed only when a
chronological held-out validation window shows it beats the physics baseline by a
margin, and the controller otherwise uses open-loop compensation. On the real
data the gate closes; in a controlled synthetic stress study we show the gate
opens exactly when a dominant systematic drift makes the residual learnable, and
then reduces simulated frequency-error, guard, and outage proxies.

This paper's primary contributions are:
\begin{itemize}
  \item \emph{A physics-first, evidence-gated uplink controller.} A
  transmitter-side LR-FHSS control framework whose default is open-loop SGP4 /
  stale-TLE Doppler compensation and whose learned residual branch is enabled
  only by a held-out evidence test, integrated at the Doppler-precompensation
  point.
  \item \emph{A rigorous negative result on real data.} Using real Space-Track
  TLE history for two BLACK~KITE satellites and strict chronological splits, we
  show learned Doppler residual correction does not beat the open-loop baseline
  at 8--168~h staleness, both target-specific and cross-satellite.
  \item \emph{A controlled characterisation of when to learn.} A synthetic stress
  study spanning noise-dominated to systematic regimes, with $\gamma$ and
  validation-window sweeps, showing the gate closes on unlearnable residuals and
  opens on dominant systematic drift.
  \item \emph{An LR-FHSS guard / outage / energy proxy formulation.} A
  frequency-error-based set of control proxies (guard-band width, frequency-miss
  outage, and energy/overhead) used to evaluate the controller without claiming
  link-layer performance.
  \item \emph{Claim--evidence discipline and limitation-aware validation.} An
  explicit binding of every claim to a software-only artifact and to allowed
  wording, keeping model-derived results strictly separate from any measured or
  RF claim (of which there are none).
\end{itemize}

% ── Related Work ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Related Work}
\label{sec:related}

\subsection{LR-FHSS for Direct-to-Satellite IoT}
LR-FHSS extends LoRaWAN for direct-to-satellite IoT by spreading transmit energy
across narrow hopping bins for Doppler diversity~\cite{semtech2023lrfhss,boquet2021lrfhss};
link-level and energy analyses quantify its behaviour under LEO Doppler, large
device populations, and header/fragment/idle
overhead~\cite{ullah2022lrfhss,hurn2023lrfhss}. A substantial
body of work then improves what happens \emph{after} a transmission exists---e.g.\
transceiver design and packet-trace receiver
studies~\cite{jung2023lrfhss,bukhari2023lrfhss}---i.e.\ synchronisation, detection,
and delivery of an existing waveform. The complementary terminal-side
question---how prediction quality should shape Doppler pre-compensation
\emph{before} transmission---is comparatively unexplored and is our focus.

\subsection{ML for LEO Orbit Prediction}
Analytical TLE propagators (SGP4/SDP4~\cite{hoots1980models,vallado2007fundamentals})
are deterministic and lightweight but accumulate timing and Doppler error with
prediction horizon and TLE age. ML orbit work increasingly treats such a
propagator as a physics anchor and learns corrections on top---Gaussian-process
correction, differentiable SGP4, and physics-informed
estimators~\cite{peng2019gp,acciarini2025dsgp4,varey2024pinn} (see~\cite{caldas2024mlorbit}
for a survey). The dominant objective there is trajectory accuracy; we instead ask
the prior question for a terminal: whether learning a Doppler residual over a
stale-TLE baseline is justified by held-out evidence \emph{at all}---and give a
real-data case where it is not.

\subsection{Uplink Control and Positioning}
LEO non-terrestrial studies characterize the strong, time-varying Doppler of LEO
passes and motivate terminal pre-compensation and timing
control~\cite{3gpp2019tr38821,hurn2023lrfhss}, and D2S uplink-policy work for
LoRa-based IoT optimizes when and how a terminal transmits from pass geometry and
link scores~\cite{alvarez2022uplink}; these policies assume a deterministic
prediction or a fixed conservative margin and do not treat trusting a
\emph{learned} correction as a gated, evidence-driven choice. Thus receiver-side
LR-FHSS work, D2S uplink policy, and ML orbit prediction expose, respectively,
post-transmission reliability, a link score or pass window, and a trajectory
estimate or residual corrector. The piece this paper addresses is the
transmitter-side decision under stale orbital information---when to deploy a
learned Doppler residual correction and when to keep the physics default---bound
to an explicit held-out evidence test, made explicit and safe-by-default. In
short, the three lines of prior art are complementary to ours along one axis:
receiver-side LR-FHSS work secures reliability \emph{after} transmission, D2S
uplink policy chooses transmission timing from a link score or pass window, and ML
orbit prediction improves the trajectory estimate---whereas we add the
transmitter-side, evidence-gated decision of \emph{when} a learned Doppler
correction should be trusted at all.

% ── Background and System Model ───────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Background and System Model}
\label{sec:system}

\subsection{LEO D2S Channel Characteristics}
In a LEO pass the one-way Doppler reaches tens of kHz (order $20$~kHz at 868~MHz,
$\sim$50~kHz at S-band), with the largest rate near closest approach and scaling
with carrier frequency and pass geometry. The Doppler shift is modeled as
\begin{equation}
f_D(t) = -\frac{v_r(t)}{c}\,f_c
\label{eq:doppler}
\end{equation}
where $v_r(t)$ is the radial velocity (positive when satellite recedes) and $c$
is the speed of light. The residual CFO after pre-compensation is
\begin{equation}
f_{\mathrm{res}}(t) = f_D(t) - \hat{f}_D(t) + f_{\mathrm{osc}},
\label{eq:residual_cfo}
\end{equation}
where $\hat{f}_D$ is the predicted Doppler and $f_{\mathrm{osc}}$ is the terminal
oscillator offset. We model reliable hop reception as $|f_{\mathrm{res}}|$ staying
small relative to a fixed frequency tolerance $F_{\mathrm{tol}}$; this is a control
proxy, not a standard-compliant receiver model.

\subsection{Stale-TLE Open-Loop Baseline and the Learned Residual}
\label{sec:baseline}
The terminal holds a TLE that is, in general, older than the present epoch.
Propagating this \emph{stale} TLE with SGP4~\cite{hoots1980models} gives the
deployable open-loop Doppler prediction $f_{\mathrm{phys}}(t)$. To study whether
this can be improved, we define a model-derived \emph{reference} Doppler
$D_{\mathrm{ref}}(t)$ as the SGP4 propagation of a \emph{later} TLE for the same
satellite at the same UTC. We emphasise that
\texttt{reference\_is\_measured\_truth = false}: $D_{\mathrm{ref}}$ is a newer
TLE solution, not an RF measurement of satellite Doppler. The quantity a learner
would have to predict is the inter-TLE residual
\begin{equation}
r(t) = D_{\mathrm{ref}}(t) - f_{\mathrm{phys}}(t),
\label{eq:residual}
\end{equation}
which encodes mean-element updates, drag-term changes, and numerical fit
differences between successive TLE solutions. A learned corrector forms
$f_{\mathrm{ml}}(t)=f_{\mathrm{phys}}(t)+\hat r(x_t)$ from a feature vector $x_t$
(TLE age, epoch gap, stale Doppler, orbital phase, geometry, and stale orbital
elements). All Doppler is computed at 868~MHz for a representative ground station
(24$^\circ$N, 121$^\circ$E).

% ── Method: Evidence Gate ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Physics-First Evidence-Gated Control}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{The Evidence Gate}
\label{sec:gate}
Let $V$ be a chronological \emph{validation} window, strictly later than the
training span and strictly earlier than the held-out test span, so that no future
TLE leaks into model selection. On $V$ we measure the mean absolute frequency
error of the physics baseline and of the learned corrector against the
model-derived reference,
\begin{align}
\mathrm{MAE}_{\mathrm{phys}}(V) &= \tfrac{1}{|V|}\textstyle\sum_{t\in V}\big|D_{\mathrm{ref}}(t)-f_{\mathrm{phys}}(t)\big|,\\
\mathrm{MAE}_{\mathrm{ml}}(V)   &= \tfrac{1}{|V|}\textstyle\sum_{t\in V}\big|D_{\mathrm{ref}}(t)-f_{\mathrm{ml}}(t)\big|.
\end{align}
The Evidence Gate enables learning only on a proven margin,
\begin{equation}
G = \mathbf{1}\!\left[\,\mathrm{MAE}_{\mathrm{ml}}(V) < \gamma\,\mathrm{MAE}_{\mathrm{phys}}(V)\,\right],
\qquad \gamma\in(0,1],
\label{eq:gate}
\end{equation}
and the deployed corrector defaults to physics otherwise,
\begin{equation}
\hat f(t) = G\,f_{\mathrm{ml}}(t) + (1-G)\,f_{\mathrm{phys}}(t).
\label{eq:deploy}
\end{equation}

\emph{Scope of the gate property (honest).} By construction
$\mathrm{MAE}_{\hat f}(V)\le \mathrm{MAE}_{\mathrm{phys}}(V)$ \emph{on the
validation window} (it equals the baseline when $G=0$ and is strictly below when
$G=1$). This is a validation-window property only, not a held-out or
distribution-shift bound: a gate-opened learner can still underperform on unseen
data. The threshold $\gamma$ trades safety against opportunism; we report the
decision across $\gamma$ and validation-window size and make no net-improvement
claim.

\subsection{Statistical Role of the Gate}
\label{sec:stat}
The gate is a binary hypothesis test on the learned corrector, and it is useful
to name its two error modes. A \emph{false open} (Type-I) occurs when the residual
is noise-dominated but a favourable validation fluctuation drives
$\mathrm{MAE}_{\mathrm{ml}}(V)$ just below $\gamma\,\mathrm{MAE}_{\mathrm{phys}}(V)$,
so $G=1$ and the deployed pre-compensation inherits avoidable prediction noise
from a corrector that has nothing real to correct. A \emph{missed open} (Type-II)
occurs when a genuine systematic drift exists but the validation estimate does not
clear the margin, so $G=0$ and the terminal forgoes a chance to shrink its
frequency margin under the proxy of Sec.~\ref{sec:proxies}. The threshold
$\gamma$ is the conservative control knob between the two: a smaller $\gamma$
demands a wider proven margin, suppressing false opens at the cost of more missed
opens, while $\gamma\!\to\!1$ is opportunistic and will open on small, possibly
spurious validation gains. We use $\gamma=0.95$ as a conservative default, which on the real BLACK~KITE data
keeps the gate closed and on the synthetic regimes opens only when the systematic
margin is large (Sec.~\ref{sec:eval}).

These error modes matter because the LEO D2S residual is unlikely to be stationary:
changing drag, orbit-determination updates over different arcs, and other unexposed
effects can shift it over time, so an offline-trained corrector may meet a
different distribution at deployment. The gate then acts as an evidence-based
circuit breaker---it re-checks the learned branch on a recent chronological window
and reverts to SGP4 / stale-TLE physics whenever that window no longer supports the
correction. The benefit is conditional and local: a validation-window decision
rule, not a promise of improvement under arbitrary distribution shift.

\subsection{LR-FHSS Guard / Outage / Energy Proxies}
\label{sec:proxies}
Residual frequency error links prediction to LR-FHSS control: since each hop
occupies a narrow sub-channel, the deployed error $e(t)=D_{\mathrm{ref}}(t)-\hat
f(t)$ sets the frequency margin to reserve and the chance a hop falls outside its
bin. From $e(t)$ we derive three control proxies used consistently across all
experiments. The \emph{guard-band proxy}
$g = 2\,p_{99}(|e|)$ is a conservative frequency-margin proxy: twice the
99th-percentile absolute error covers the bulk of the error distribution while
remaining a percentile statistic rather than an absolute extremum. The
\emph{outage proxy} $\rho = \Pr[\,|e| > F_{\mathrm{tol}}\,]$, with a fixed
bin-tolerance $F_{\mathrm{tol}}=500$~Hz, represents the frequency-miss probability
under a simplified bin-tolerance model. The \emph{energy/overhead proxy} is
monotone in the reserved margin and in the repeated-transmission pressure that
missed hops induce; a convenient dimensionless form is
\begin{equation}
E_{\mathrm{proxy}} \;\propto\; \Big(1 + \alpha_g\,\tfrac{g}{B}\Big)\,(1 + \rho),
\label{eq:eproxy}
\end{equation}
where $B$ is the hopping bandwidth and $\alpha_g\!\ge\!0$ weights idle guard
against retransmission pressure. Equation~\eqref{eq:eproxy} is an illustrative
overhead \emph{proxy}, not a measured energy or packet-delivery model. All three
are control-layer proxies for terminal margin sizing---explicitly \emph{not}
packet-error, bit-error, or link-budget measurements.

The proxies also motivate a \emph{pass-aware} guard. The Doppler rate
$\dot f_D(t)$ varies strongly across a pass, peaking near closest approach, so a
residual timing offset $\Delta t$ couples into a frequency error
\begin{equation}
\Delta f_D(t) \;\approx\; \dot f_D(t)\,\Delta t,
\label{eq:ratecoupling}
\end{equation}
i.e.\ the same timing slack costs more frequency margin where the rate is high.
This favours sizing the guard along the pass---wider near the high-rate portion,
narrower where geometry is slow---rather than one fixed margin. We use
Eq.~\eqref{eq:ratecoupling} as control-proxy motivation only and claim no
timing-synchronisation or measured packet-recovery performance.

\subsection{Datasets and Splits}
\label{sec:data}
Real evaluation uses Space-Track TLE history for two BLACK~KITE LEO satellites:
BLACK~KITE-1 (NORAD~66741, 415 records, 2025-12-18 to 2026-06-12, median
inter-TLE gap $6.3$~h) and BLACK~KITE-2 (NORAD~68474, 184 records, 2026-04-13 to
2026-06-13, median gap $6.4$~h). For each reference (newer) TLE, an older TLE
inside a target staleness band is paired with it, propagated open-loop, and
compared to the reference at 24 UTC samples over one orbital period; pairs with a
manoeuvre/bad-fit outlier are rejected. Splits are chronological by reference
epoch (60/20/20 train/validation/test), so the stale TLE in any test pair is
always available before the test reference epoch. The synthetic stress study
(Sec.~\ref{sec:eval-stress}) is a controlled simulation and uses no Space-Track
data.

\emph{End-to-end procedure (compact).} At each control epoch the terminal:
(1)~SGP4-propagates the stale TLE to $f_{\mathrm{phys}}(t)$ and forms
$f_{\mathrm{ml}}(t)=f_{\mathrm{phys}}(t)+\hat r(x_t)$; (2)~evaluates
$\mathrm{MAE}_{\mathrm{phys}}(V),\mathrm{MAE}_{\mathrm{ml}}(V)$ on the chronological
window $V$; (3)~sets $G$ by Eq.~\eqref{eq:gate} and deploys $\hat f$ by
Eq.~\eqref{eq:deploy}; and (4)~sizes the guard from $g=2\,p_{99}(|e|)$. It needs no
receiver feedback and is recomputed whenever a fresh TLE arrives, so the gate
tracks the most recent on-board evidence.

% ── Evaluation ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:eval}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
Doppler is computed with python-SGP4 at 868~MHz for the representative ground
station of Sec.~\ref{sec:baseline}. The 868~MHz carrier is representative for
Doppler scaling and reproducibility; the controller is frequency-parametric
(Eq.~\eqref{eq:doppler}) and applies at other carriers by rescaling $f_c$, with no
regulatory assumption. Learned candidates are ridge, random forest,
gradient boosting, and a small MLP, selected on the chronological validation
segment with the gate evaluated once on the held-out test segment; a window counts
as supported only if the selected model beats the zero-residual baseline on
held-out MAE by a clear margin and also improves RMSE. All results here are
software-only and model-derived; no hardware, RF, UART, replay, or measured
Doppler is involved.

\subsection{Real BLACK~KITE Negative Result}
\label{sec:eval-real}
Table~\ref{tab:bk} reports the held-out test MAE of the validation-selected
learned model against the zero-residual stale-TLE baseline. The learned residual
is worse than the baseline at \emph{every} tested staleness, both target-specific
(BK1, 8--168~h) and cross-satellite (BK1$\rightarrow$BK2), so the gate of
Eq.~\eqref{eq:gate} \emph{closes} in all cases. The held-out residual is
near zero-mean (e.g.\ BK1 test residual mean $|{\cdot}|\!\lesssim\!0.1$~Hz across
windows) with a spread that grows with staleness but no learnable structure: the
inter-TLE refresh ($\sim$6~h median) leaves little to correct at short staleness,
and BK1 manoeuvre/orbit-determination outliers make cross-satellite transfer
actively harmful. This is the paper's central real-data finding: on these
satellites, learning does not help, and the safe physics default is correct.
Fig.~\ref{fig:bkres} visualises this: panel~(a) plots held-out MAE vs.\ staleness
with the learned curve \emph{above} the baseline throughout, and panel~(b) shows
the relative degradation $\Delta\%$ staying negative at every staleness, for both
the target-specific and cross-satellite settings.

This is \emph{consistent with} how the data is produced: SGP4 is a lightweight
analytical propagator fitted through TLE mean elements, and successive Space-Track
TLEs reflect orbit-determination updates over different observation arcs, so the
inter-TLE residual may mix model-fit artifacts, drag-model mismatch, tracking/fit
noise, and occasional maneuver-like or bad-fit events. From the terminal-feature
perspective these appear weakly structured and near zero-mean, so a regressor may
fit validation noise rather than a stable correction---without asserting that one
mechanism dominates, that the residual is white for every satellite, or that the
propagator is globally best. Read constructively, it is a rigorous real-data case
for why always-on residual learning is unsafe when a terminal cannot verify a
correction before transmitting, and hence why an evidence gate is the appropriate
design; richer features or longer staleness could still expose learnable structure,
precisely the case the gate is built to admit.

\subsection{Controlled Synthetic Stress: When the Gate Opens}
\label{sec:eval-stress}
To characterise the gate mechanism we run a controlled synthetic simulation
(no Space-Track data, no measurement): the residual is generated as
$r = A\,s(x) + \mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2)$ with a fixed nonlinear systematic
function $s(\cdot)$, and $(A,\sigma)$ chosen per regime to bracket gate
behaviour. Table~\ref{tab:stress} summarises three regimes. In the
noise-dominated \texttt{fresh\_low\_residual} regime---which matches the real
BLACK~KITE residual structure---the learned model cannot beat the baseline and
the gate closes. In the \texttt{extreme\_systematic} regime a dominant learnable
drift exists, the learned model sharply cuts test MAE, guard-band, and outage
(Table~\ref{tab:stress}), and the gate opens. The \texttt{moderate\_staleness}
regime is marginal: the learned model is only $\sim$5\% better on test, so at
$\gamma=0.95$ the gate stays closed rather than enable an unproven learner. The
systematic-regime guard reduction is an energy/overhead \emph{proxy} from the
deployed frequency error---not a measured power saving, gateway search-window, or
link-layer result---and appears only in this synthetic regime, absent from the real
BLACK~KITE data.

\subsection{Threshold and Validation-Window Sensitivity}
\label{sec:eval-sens}
Fig.~\ref{fig:gate} plots, against the threshold $\gamma$, the gate decision (top)
and the resulting deployed MAE (bottom) for the three regimes. The two decisive
regimes are insensitive to $\gamma$: the noise-dominated regime stays closed and
the strongly-systematic regime stays open across $\gamma\in[0.90,1.00]$. Only the
marginal regime is threshold-sensitive, flipping closed$\to$open between
$\gamma=0.95$ and $0.99$. A validation-window sweep gives the same decisions across
$n\in\{100,300,1000,3000\}$; only very small windows ($n\!\approx\!100$) can
mis-decide near the threshold. The real BK1/BK2 validation segments sit deep in the
noise-dominated regime, so the real-data decision (closed) is unambiguous; we adopt
$\gamma=0.95$.

\subsection{Implications and Design Rules}
\label{sec:implications}
Reading Table~\ref{tab:bk}, Fig.~\ref{fig:gate}, and Table~\ref{tab:stress}
together yields a consistent rule. In
noise-dominated regimes---which the real BLACK~KITE data exhibits---the gate closes
and the terminal keeps the open-loop SGP4 / stale-TLE baseline; an always-on
learner would only add noise (the false-open mode of Sec.~\ref{sec:stat}). In the
systematic synthetic regime the gate opens and the learned branch shrinks the
guard-band proxy from $\approx 14.1$~kHz to $\approx 950$~Hz and the outage proxy
from $0.814$ to $0.008$, so a terminal with logged evidence can reserve a much
smaller margin. The moderate regime shows why the threshold matters: a $\sim$5\%
validation gain does not open at $\gamma=0.95$. Because the gate's reliability
grows with the amount of accumulated chronological evidence (the validation-window
sweep), the controller is data-dependent by construction.

These observations translate into concrete design rules. In a \emph{fresh /
frequently refreshed} TLE regime---both BLACK~KITE feeds refresh on a $\sim$6~h
cadence---the open-loop prediction is already accurate and a learned residual
should not be deployed unconditionally; in a \emph{stale or biased} regime the
residual \emph{may} be learnable, but only after a validation window clears the
margin $\gamma$; and in the \emph{moderate} regime $\gamma$ is the explicit
conservatism knob, with reliability growing with the validation-window size
(Sec.~\ref{sec:eval-sens}). The terminal should therefore treat the gate as an
auditable control component, logging per control epoch the decision $G$ and
$\gamma$, the validation MAE pair $(\mathrm{MAE}_{\mathrm{phys}}(V),\mathrm{MAE}_{\mathrm{ml}}(V))$
with $|V|$, and the stale-TLE age---keeping the learned branch reversible and the
``when to learn'' decision reproducible. These are practical rules from a
software-only study, not a universal performance claim.

% ── Limitations and Future Work ──────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Limitations and Future Work}
\label{sec:limits}

This study is software-only and model-derived. All ``reference'' Doppler is SGP4
from real TLE (\texttt{reference\_is\_measured\_truth=false})---there is no measured
Doppler truth, no live-satellite contact, no standards-compliant LR-FHSS decoding,
and no packet-error rate (PER), bit-error rate (BER), cyclic redundancy check
(CRC), packet-delivery ratio (PDR), or gateway acknowledgement. The negative result
holds for two BLACK~KITE satellites and our feature set, not universally; the
positive learning result is synthetic only (Sec.~\ref{sec:eval-stress}); the gate
provides a validation-window property (Sec.~\ref{sec:gate}), not a held-out or
distribution-shift safety property; and guard/outage/energy are control proxies,
not link-budget or packet-delivery measurements.

\emph{Hardware-in-the-loop attempt.} A supervised Mac conducted hardware-in-the-loop
attempt was carried out and \emph{halted as inconclusive}: the physical bench
lacked an SMA/u.FL conducted RF path and a fixed attenuator between the LR1121
transmit output and the USRP~B210 receive input (the boards were only co-located,
with a LoRa antenna unsuitable as conducted evidence), so a TX-ON capture was not
distinguishable from the TX-OFF baseline. We therefore claim \emph{no} conducted
RF signal presence and no hardware validation from this attempt; the requirements
for a valid future run are set out next, and closing this conducted-IQ and
receiver-in-the-loop gap is the primary future work.

\emph{Future conducted-HIL roadmap.} A valid conducted bench needs three setup
conditions: (i)~a \emph{conducted RF loop}---LR1121 RF output via u.FL/SMA coax
into the B210 RX, with no radiated LoRa antenna as evidence; (ii)~\emph{calibrated
attenuation}---a fixed $50\,\Omega$ attenuator keeping the B210 input in its linear
region to avoid ADC clipping; and (iii)~\emph{clock/CFO discipline}---a shared
reference or explicit CFO estimation, since independent oscillators can mimic
frequency structure. Evidence should then be reported at three separated levels:
L1, IQ-level TX-ON/OFF signal presence (DC/LO/image excluded); L2, hop-structure /
residual-CFO / occupancy \emph{proxies} (not decoding); and L3, gateway-class
decoded packet-level metrics. None is performed or claimed here; the roadmap states
only what a future hardware study would need to move from model-derived to measured
results.

% ── Conclusion ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Conclusion}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We presented a physics-first, evidence-gated controller for transmitter-side
LR-FHSS Doppler pre-compensation under stale orbital information: open-loop SGP4 is
the safe default, and a learned residual is deployed only when a chronological
held-out window beats the baseline by a margin. On real BLACK~KITE TLE history the
learned correction never beats the baseline (gate closes); in a controlled
synthetic study it opens only under dominant systematic drift. All results are
software-only and model-derived; closing the conducted-IQ and receiver-in-the-loop
gap on a properly cabled, attenuated bench is future work.
