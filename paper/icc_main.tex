\title{Orbit-Evidence: Relational Validity Checks for\\
Learning-Assisted Satellite Communication Experiments}

\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Anonymous Submission}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Chronological train/validation/test splitting constrains temporal order, but it does not establish
every condition a valid deployment claim requires. Some such conditions are not properties of a
single run at all: they are relations across executions or across aggregation levels. We introduce
\emph{Orbit-Evidence}, an executable validity framework for learning-assisted satellite
communication experiments. Its two central relational checks make provenance
incompleteness differentially falsifiable, and test for residual dependence at the next coarser
physical level---the latter against a permutation reference admitting a third outcome,
\textsc{indeterminate}, when the design cannot support a decision. Satellite software makes these
obligations concrete: element epoch versus catalogue publication time, predicted visibility,
retrospectively closing labels, state persisting across a freeze, and pass-level dependence. The
calibrated unit check has a measured false-halt rate of $\artv{l47_rate}{0.031}$, Wilson
$\artv{l47_wilson}{[0.018,0.052]}$, consistent with a nominal $\artv{l47_alpha}{0.05}$ rather than
better, and is then applied unchanged to real catalogue records for
\artv{real_objects}{\num{11}} objects, where a fit-update observable shows within-group dependence and a geometric one does not reject. On a
separately frozen third-party spacecraft-telemetry artifact the unmodified contract identifies
\artv{ext_halt}{three} violations and \artv{ext_indet}{one} indeterminate condition; correcting one
pre-registered validation-overlap violation changes the selected model in all
\artv{cons_seeds}{five} paired runs, while the downstream endpoint is not estimable at the available
paired-run resolution. A curated \artv{fault_class_count}{seventeen}-fault suite supplies regression coverage
of the represented violations. We claim no completeness, no radio-frequency result, and no
improvement in learned-method accuracy.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\input{figures/fig_architecture.tex}

Learning components are increasingly proposed as assistants to physical models in satellite
and non-terrestrial communication software: correcting a propagated orbital prediction,
adapting a link parameter, or deciding whether a learned branch should override a model-based
default~\cite{lin2021ntn}. Because such systems are temporal, evaluation is
organised as a chronological split---train on the past, validate on the near future, test on
the far future. That is a common and necessary practice for temporal learning pipelines, and
the conditions under which ordered splitting is valid are well
established~\cite{bergmeir2012crossval}.

Our claim is that it is not sufficient, and that the gap is one of kind rather than degree. A
chronological split constrains the \emph{order} of quantities already in a dataset, and is silent
on four further questions. \emph{Availability}: a quantity may be past-dated and still
unpublished, so no system could have held it. \emph{Membership}: which rows exist can itself be
decided by later data, in both directions, and a split cannot examine rows never created.
\emph{Hidden state}: a split governs rows, whereas information travels through a feature tensor,
a scaler, a coefficient vector, a tracker's latent state, selection metadata, or an admission
bit---none of which is a column. \emph{Statistical units}: a split says nothing about whether the unit over
which uncertainty is computed is exchangeable.

These four share a structure, and the structure matters more than the list. A chronological split
is a \emph{predicate over the rows of one realised dataset}: given the table, its truth is
decidable. The four questions are not. Whether a feature was obtainable depends on a source state
the realised run does not contain; whether row membership was future-independent depends on a
registry rebuilt from a truncated source; whether hidden state crossed the freeze depends on a
second execution; whether the chosen unit is exchangeable depends on a coarser aggregation level.
Each has a counterexample that exists only outside the dataset in hand. We therefore distinguish
\emph{row-local} validity, evaluable from one realised run, from \emph{relational} validity, whose
truth depends on a relation between two executions, two source states, or two aggregation levels.
This distinction is the paper's foundation: chronological splitting constrains the order of
realised observations and cannot, by construction, establish a property whose counterexample lives
in another execution, another source state, or another aggregation level.

Three obvious repairs do not close the gap. A \emph{stricter split} leaves past-dated but
unpublished information past-dated, cannot examine rows that were never created, and does not
represent model state as rows. \emph{More single-run assertions} cannot falsify a relation whose
counterexample requires a second execution. And \emph{richer provenance logging} cannot establish
its own completeness: a manifest may record every field its author recalled and still omit a
behaviour-changing dependency. The gap is structural, so the response has to change what is
checked rather than how much.

\subsection{Why the satellite domain makes this concrete}
\label{sec:whysat}

Satellite software supplies each of these obligations in operational form, which is why we develop
the contract here rather than as generic tooling. \emph{(i) Two clocks}: an element set has an
\emph{element epoch}, the instant its state vector describes, and a separate message-creation
stamp, \texttt{CREATION\_DATE}, exposed per element set by the public
catalogue~\cite{ccsds502omm,spacetrackgp}---an optimistic \emph{lower bound} on retrievability, not
the retrieval instant. They differ by an object-level median of
\qty{\artv{lag_object_median_h}{6.36}}{\hour} with a tail to days, so a
feature formed from two element epochs is a difference of two past timestamps---it passes every
ordering test and is still unavailable if either was published after the decision instant.
\emph{(ii) Row membership is generated by physics}: an opportunity exists only where the geometry is
predicted above an elevation mask from \emph{held} state, so a uniform grid filtered afterwards yields
rows no scheduler would emit. \emph{(iii) Labels arrive retrospectively}, and whether and when one
appears is the publisher's decision. \emph{(iv) Repeated measures nest}: every pass propagated from
one element set inherits that set's largely along-track fit signature, so the pass is too fine a unit
whenever the observable carries it---\S\ref{sec:external} measures this, and finds the element set
too fine as well. \emph{(v) State survives
a nominal freeze}, stateful trackers refreshing at their own events, which can fall after a model is
declared frozen.

We therefore treat experimental validity as an \emph{executable contract}: each rule protects a
named object, states a violation, and is enforced by a check naming the rule it broke.

\textbf{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Two relational validity checks.} \emph{Differential provenance completeness}
(\rulename{L4.6}): rather than enumerating the dependencies a manifest might omit, we test a
necessary relation---if behaviour changes, declared provenance must change---so two runs differing
in output under an identical manifest hash constitute a counterexample. Failure proves
incompleteness; success does not prove completeness, and that asymmetry is what makes the property
falsifiable without enumerating the input space. \emph{Statistical-unit adequacy}
(\rulename{L4.7}): choosing an aggregation level does not establish that it is exchangeable, so we
test whether dependence survives one declared physical level coarser, calibrated against a
permutation reference under the realised grouping geometry, and \emph{abstain} when that geometry
cannot attain the nominal level. Insufficient resolution is not evidence of validity.
\item \textbf{A satellite experiment contract that instantiates them.} The two checks sit among
nineteen executable rules in four layers (Fig.~\ref{fig:contract},
Table~\ref{tab:rules}) that map the two clocks, row membership, label closure, persistent state and the
statistical unit onto the objects a deployment claim depends on. The catalogue is the
instantiation, not the novelty: three further rules are relational recastings of practice we cite,
and the remainder are row-local obligations that make the relational ones checkable at all.
Implemented as the \artv{source_loc}{\num{833}}-line \texttt{numpy}-only Orbit-Evidence toolkit.
\item \textbf{Calibrated, real-data and third-party evidence.} We measure the unit gate's operating
characteristic (Fig.~\ref{fig:opcurve}), apply the frozen rule to real orbital records for
\artv{real_objects}{\num{11}} objects, and run the unmodified contract on a separately frozen public
spacecraft-telemetry artifact, where \artv{ext_halt}{three} rules halt and
\artv{ext_nobs}{\num{5}} of \artv{ext_classified}{\num{19}} are recorded \emph{not observable}
rather than passed. A pre-registered correction of one detected violation changes model selection in
all five paired runs; the downstream endpoint is not estimable at the available paired-run
resolution.
\end{enumerate}

We claim nothing about radio-frequency or packet-level performance. The object of study is the
experiment, not the model.

\emph{Availability.} The toolkit, fixtures, fault suite and summary artifact accompany this
submission anonymously. \texttt{make gate} regenerates the matrix and \rulename{L4.7}'s operating
characteristic, and refuses to build if a headline number \emph{disagrees} with the artifact at the
sentence claiming it, if such a sentence is missing, or if a retracted claim reappears. The two
studies of \S\ref{sec:external} are hash-verified rather than regenerated, their inputs not being
distributable anonymously. It is a regression guard, not a proof of correctness.

\subsection{The decision boundary and the protected objects}

Let $t_d$ be the \emph{decision instant}: the moment a deployed system must act using only state it
already holds---for an open-loop satellite endpoint, when a transmission is committed from the
element set on board. A deployment claim is interpretable only if every quantity entering the
decision was obtainable before $t_d$. The objects that must therefore be protected follow from
\S\ref{sec:whysat}, and Fig.~\ref{fig:contract} places them against the experiment and the layers
that check them.

\emph{Feature availability} requires a feature computable from state available at $t_d$, and the
\emph{availability clock} selects it on the message-creation bound rather than on the descriptive
epoch. \emph{Row membership} may have its label status,
value, uncertainty or closure time changed by later information, but never its existence; violations
run both ways---rows dropped when a later reference fails to appear, rows created when a schedule
takes its extent from the data on hand. \emph{Label closure} makes a retrospective label usable only
from $t_c$, and use before it is one of the two ordering violations a split does catch. The harder failure is when the \emph{probability} that a label exists depends
on the covariate under study. \emph{State channels} carry information that is not a column---feature tensor, scaler,
coefficients, a tracker's latent state, selected-model metadata~\cite{cawley2010selection}, the gate
bit---six probed channels, an operational enumeration rather than a completeness claim. And the
\emph{statistical unit} must be a level at which replicates of one physical event are exchangeable,
which aggregating somewhere does not establish.

\subsection{Four layers}

Fig.~\ref{fig:contract} places the layers against the experiment they observe and
Table~\ref{tab:rules} gives their targets and decision semantics. Two commitments make the
catalogue usable. The protected objects do not partition the rules: six enforce one
directly---\rulename{L1.1}--\rulename{L1.4}, \rulename{L3.1}, \rulename{L4.7}---and the rest are
what those obligations need to be checkable at all. And a rule is trusted only once demonstrated to
fail on a deliberately broken fixture, since a check that cannot go red is not evidence.

\subsection{Primitives, prior art, and what is method}

We propose none of the primitives: $\mathrm{ICC}(1)$~\cite{shrout1979icc},
permutation inference over exchangeability blocks~\cite{winkler2014permutation}, metamorphic
relations over runs~\cite{chen2018metamorphic}, hermetic dependency
closure~\cite{mokhov2018buildsystems}, mutation testing~\cite{jia2011mutation}, hashing, and
point-in-time correctness in feature stores. Nor is the territory: executable
production-readiness rubrics~\cite{breck2017mltestscore,polyzotis2019datavalidation}, hidden-state
leakage as configuration coupling~\cite{sculley2015debt},
pseudoreplication~\cite{hurlbert1984pseudoreplication}, boundary-value analysis, leakage
taxonomies~\cite{kaufman2012leakage} with prevalence surveys~\cite{kapoor2023leakage}, and
time-series results on when ordered splitting is valid~\cite{bergmeir2012crossval} all predate us.

What is method rather than primitive is the conversion: identify which validity obligations are
relational, choose the counterfactual execution or coarser physical level that makes each
falsifiable, encode the resulting invariant as a contract, and let an undecidable case return
\textsc{indeterminate} rather than pass. Concretely, hermetic builds test dependency closure over a
build; we transfer that obligation to experiment provenance, where tracking systems record declared
parameters without testing whether the declaration is behaviourally complete. And a permutation
reference is ordinarily an analysis step; we use it to calibrate a \emph{gate}, with an operating
characteristic and an abstention state. Two smaller debts: selection on a validation window is
itself a leakage channel~\cite{cawley2010selection}, which is why selected-model metadata is a
probed state channel, and binding each number to its claiming sentence is weaker than literate
computing~\cite{knuth1984literate}, which makes disagreement impossible rather than
merely detected. Manifests follow the discipline
urged for datasets~\cite{gebru2021datasheets}; element conventions are
standard~\cite{vallado2006revisiting}.

\section{Orbit-Evidence Toolkit}



\subsection{The two relational checks, formally}

One judgement generates both. Neither obligation can be \emph{certified}---enumerating the
dependencies a manifest might omit is hopeless, and no finite design proves exchangeability---so we
accept checks that can only \emph{refuse}, testing a necessary consequence instead. A failure then carries the weight---a counterexample for \rulename{L4.6}, a rejection at the
nominal level for \rulename{L4.7}---and a success carries none; the same stance forbids collapsing
an undecidable design into \textsc{pass}.

\emph{Provenance completeness} (\rulename{L4.6}). Over input variations $\{c_i\}$ with output
digests $o_i$ and manifest hashes $h_i$, any pair with $o_i \neq o_j$ and $h_i = h_j$ proves the
manifest incomplete, since two runs differing in output must differ in provenance. The converse is
redundant rather than erroneous, and that one-sided implication is the whole mechanism.

\emph{Statistical-unit adequacy} (\rulename{L4.7}). Given values $y$, unit identifiers $u$ and a
declared coarser grouping $g$, we aggregate to $\bar{y}_u$ and measure its intraclass correlation
\emph{within} $g$: residual correlation there means the chosen unit shares state with its
neighbours, so intervals over it are optimistic.

The estimator is the one-way $\mathrm{ICC}(1)$ from variance
components~\cite{shrout1979icc}, whose numerator subtracts the within-group mean square and so has
expectation $0$ under independence at every group size, reported truncated at zero, and the
decision is referenced to a \emph{permutation null}: unit values are permuted across coarser
groups---the exchangeability hypothesis under test---giving
$p=(1+\#\{\hat{\rho}^{*}\ge\hat{\rho}\})/(B+1)$ over $B=\num{400}$ permutations, halting at
$p\le\alpha=0.05$. The $+1$ is what makes the test valid: without it the empirical $1-\alpha$ quantile is anticonservative
by $1/(B+1)$, this form valid for any $B$. The reference distribution, not the estimator,
calibrates the decision: under that null a raw scatter ratio is a monotone transform of
$\hat{\rho}$ and gives the same $p$. We calibrate the operating characteristic directly: over
\artv{l47_clean_paths}{\num{450}} clean evaluations it halts \artv{l47_false_halts}{\num{14}}
times, $\artv{l47_rate}{0.031}$, Wilson~\cite{wilson1927}
$\artv{l47_wilson}{[0.018,0.052]}$, rounded outward---consistent with the nominal $\artv{l47_alpha}{0.05}$, not
better---and detects the injected fault \artv{l47_injected}{\num{150}} of \artv{l47_injected}{\num{150}} times. All \artv{l47_clean_paths}{\num{450}} replicates sit
at one design whose clean fixture satisfies the null by construction, so this measures the
\emph{implementation}, and the interval rather than the point is the claim. Specificity alone would
hide that the rule finds gross unit errors and misses subtle ones. Finally the rule \emph{declines to judge} below four coarser groups of two or more units, or
eight units in all. That floor is an \emph{attainability} precondition, not a power one: three groups of two
admit only $6!/(2!^{3}3!)=15$ distinct assignments, so the smallest reachable $p$ is
$1/15=0.067>\alpha$ and no design that small can reject at all, whereas four groups of two admit
$105$ and so reach $1/105=0.0095$. Those are the designs' \emph{exact} resolutions; the rule never
enumerates them, always drawing the same $B$. The rule is therefore three-valued:
\textsc{halt}, the condition is violated; \textsc{indeterminate}, the design cannot support the
decision; and \textsc{pass}, which means \emph{not rejected}, not verified---at power $\artv{l47_power_02}{0.25}$ for
$\rho=0.2$ three genuinely dependent designs in four also pass. Collapsing \textsc{indeterminate}
into \textsc{pass} is how a design that could not have rejected comes to look like one that did not. No condition in our own suite reaches
the floor; the third-party artifact of \S\ref{sec:external} does.

\emph{Canaries and seeds.} The probed state channels separate whether a channel is \emph{guarded} from whether the probe was \emph{effective}, since an inert mutation reads as an undetectable leak (\rulename{L3.2}). Seeds live in three disjoint namespaces, one whose outcomes have been inspected is burned, and compared conditions draw one seed per invariant cell, pairing asserted on the realised arrays.

\section{Curated Regression Evaluation}
\label{sec:eval}

Supporting regression evidence, not the novelty result. Two minimal deterministic fixtures modelled
on the retrospective chains of \S\ref{sec:external} exercise a retrospective-label chain and a
learning-and-gating chain; neither carries a physical claim. The suite holds
\artv{fault_class_count}{\num{17}} \emph{curated} fault classes drawn from the defects that
motivated the rules, each injected in three environments differing in generator family, plus a clean

\artv{rule_count}{\num{19}} \emph{rules}, \artv{fault_class_count}{\num{17}} fault \emph{classes}, \artv{conditions}{\num{18}}

asserting ordered disjoint fold extents, no row in a fold whose span excludes it, and no label used

\section{Evidence From Outside This Project}
\label{sec:external}

Everything to this point was produced here; the records and the artifact below were not. Table~\ref{tab:external} collects it.

\emph{Does the unit problem exist in real satellite data?} Yes. Applying the frozen
\rulename{L4.7} rule to real Space-Track records, pass rows carrying the in-track update increment,
grouped by their generating element set, yield $\hat{\rho}=\artv{at_icc_d1}{0.501}$, permutation
$p=\artv{at_p}{0.0025}$, one-sided $95\%$ lower bound $\artv{at_icc_d1_lo}{0.376}$, and the rule
\textsc{halts}. Grouping this increment by object gives $\artv{at_icc_d2}{0.241}$; grouping element sets within
objects gives $\artv{at_icc_d3}{0.284}$. Both also halt, all three at the smallest $p$ the
reference admits at $B=\num{400}$, so the element set is not exchangeable either and no level tested
here is. For this pass-row construction, exchangeability of samples sharing a
generating element set is rejected---which is what \S\ref{sec:whysat}\,iv asserts.

The analysis covers \artv{real_objects}{\num{11}} objects, \artv{real_elsets}{\num{109}} element
sets and \artv{real_passes}{\num{331}} visible passes over one declared \textsc{wgs}-84 ground station at
\ang{\artv{st_lat}{24.7961}}\,N, \ang{\artv{st_lon}{120.9967}}\,E above a
\ang{\artv{st_mask}{10}} mask, sampled at \qty{\artv{st_dt}{30}}{\second} over
\qty{\artv{st_horizon}{24}}{\hour} from each element set's epoch, with \textsc{sgp4} supplying geometry only. Unit
counts are \artv{real_units_a}{\num{101}} and \artv{real_units_b}{\num{305}} rather than
\artv{real_elsets}{\num{109}} and \artv{real_passes}{\num{331}}: an element set republished
under a new catalogue identifier keeps its epoch, so the republished records and the passes they
generate collapse onto units already present. Of the \artv{real_passes}{\num{331}} passes,
\artv{at_dropped}{\num{59}} are dropped for having no successor element set, leaving
\artv{at_passes}{\num{272}} over \artv{at_elsets}{\num{90}} element sets for the along-track
analysis.

Two limits bound this. The differenced quantity is an \emph{update increment}---the in-track
component of the difference between consecutive fits both propagated to the pass midpoint---not an
error against truth, hence its median of only
\qty{\artv{at_median_km}{0.226}}{\kilo\metre}; its \qty{\artv{at_max_km}{559.022}}{\kilo\metre} tail is manoeuvre-scale and unscreened. And reversing the declared coarser
level changes the statistic but not the decision, so the rule adjudicates whether a declared level
is adequate, never which is right.

\emph{The observable determines what the rule can say.} Grouping element sets by object on
publication lag, and passes by element set on mean elevation, both passed at
$\hat{\rho}=\artv{real_icc_ab}{0.000},\ p=\artv{real_p_ab}{1.000}$ against our registered halts---a ties signature, the estimator
truncating at zero, so not an effect size. Mean elevation is near-invariant to which element set propagated a
pass, and publication lag is a catalogue property; neither carries the fit-update signature. \artv{real_halt}{One} of eleven per-object tests halted and
\artv{real_pass}{10} passed, but $P(\ge 1)$ under the global null across those
\artv{at_decisions}{fourteen} decisions---eleven per object and three pooled---is
$\artv{at_p_any}{0.512}$.

\emph{Can the frozen contract operate outside the project that built it?} Yes. On a public
spacecraft-telemetry \textsc{lstm} artifact~\cite{hundman2018telemanom} frozen at a commit chosen
before any inspection, the unchanged contract returns the outcomes of
Table~\ref{tab:external}. A \emph{rule verdict} is
three-valued and is what a rule returns once its obligation applies; auditing adds two
\emph{dispositions} preceding any verdict---\emph{not applicable}, the obligation does not arise,
and \emph{not observable}, it arises but the inspected artifact does not expose the inputs the rule
needs. The second is never scored as compliance. Selection criteria were fixed
before searching and no detector was touched: the contract's \texttt{sha256} is identical before
and after, and equal to the pre-registration hash. The telemetry arrays were obtained from a
checksum-verified mirror after the upstream endpoint became unavailable; per-file hashes matched two
independently published checksum sources. That is mirror concordance, not verification by the
original publisher.

The halt with methodological weight is \rulename{L4.1}: overlapping windows sharing \artv{cons_windows}{\num{259}} of
\artv{cons_span}{\num{260}} samples are shuffled and then split by fraction, so the early-stopping validation set is
drawn from the training window's own time span. We scope it to that signal rather than to the
reported test results. The other two are provenance
obligations research code is not generally written to satisfy---a declared configuration value the
implementation may silently reduce, and no hash binding a run to its inputs. Where provenance is incomplete we record
that the artifact does not expose enough, never that its authors erred.

\rulename{L4.7} also ran on the artifact's per-channel errors and \emph{abstained} at the grouping
its authors declare---two spacecraft, below its floor---then decided at a finer grouping the
artifact exposes: the abstention semantics behaving as specified on data nobody here produced.

\emph{Does a detected violation matter?} We pre-registered one intervention, froze the detector's
hash, then corrected only the partition. Carving the validation block chronologically and dropping
the \num{259} windows that make source-timestep overlap impossible takes overlap from
\qty{\artv{cons_overlap_pct}{100}}{\percent} of the validation support to zero and \rulename{L4.1}
from halt to pass. Across \artv{cons_seeds}{\num{5}} paired seeds it changed which model early
stopping selected \artv{cons_sel_changed}{\num{5}} times out of five, every checkpoint differing;
re-running one seed under identical conditions reproduces its checkpoint bit for bit, so this is the
intervention and not training noise. The reported detection metric moved on three seeds in
\emph{both} directions: an anomaly was flagged in \artv{cons_det_orig}{3} of five seeds before the
correction and \artv{cons_det_corr}{4} after, one seed losing a detection and two gaining one. We do not call that a null: with five paired seeds the smallest attainable
two-sided $p$ is $\artv{cons_minp}{0.0625}$, above $\alpha$, so by the same attainability argument
that makes \rulename{L4.7} abstain, this endpoint is \emph{not estimable at the available paired-run
resolution}---a disposition of this study's evidence, not a rule verdict---and the channel compounds
it, its target taking one value in training and two in test. Selection consequence demonstrated;
downstream consequence not estimable. We claim nothing about the upstream paper's
validity or about improving anomaly detection.

\section{Threats to Validity, Limitations and Conclusion}
\label{sec:threats}

\emph{Evaluation scope.} The curated fault suite is primarily regression evidence. Faults and rules largely share an
origin---one programme, mostly one hand---so $17/17$ measures detector
\emph{reachability}~\cite{jia2011mutation}, and hand-authored mutants overstate suite
strength~\cite{just2014mutants}. Most mutated objects are consumed only by another check, never by
a computation producing a reported number---the sharpest limit here; \rulename{L2.2}, \rulename{L2.3} and \rulename{L4.5} fire in no curated
condition, though \rulename{L4.5} did halt on the third-party artifact. Only one fault was specified
before any detector existed. We therefore make no unseen-fault sensitivity claim: represented
violations, once fixed, do not silently return in the form the suite injects. The third-party study partially breaks the loop but remains one artifact and one channel,
and the curated environments differ in generator family alone.

\emph{Contract scope.} Several rules give weaker guarantees than their names suggest, deliberately.
\rulename{L4.6} is bounded by the caller's variation set, so the input nobody thought of is absent
from the sweep by construction. \rulename{L4.3} is a heuristic guard on a self-reported flag,
its fixed threshold halting about one correct design in six at eight groups. \rulename{L4.5} checks
that a manifest and self-hash exist, not that either is correct. \rulename{L4.7} adjudicates only
the grouping supplied to it, has power against gross unit errors, and presumes a \emph{unique} next
coarser level that orbital data does not supply, since element sets and deployment episodes
cross-cut. Four obligations remain outside the contract: $t_d$ is declared rather than
checked; covariate-coupled label availability has no rule, found by an \emph{ad hoc} diagnostic and
never promoted; exact retrieval time is unavailable, and we observed the same element set republished under a new identifier with a later
stamp; and common-mode effects are unmodelled, drag driven by solar and geomagnetic activity being
shared across every object and element set in an interval. Epoch~$\le$~publication is likewise
sometimes false, in \artv{timing_outlier_objects}{one} of \artv{lag_objects}{\num{11}} objects over
its full history---reported per object rather than pooled over
\artv{lag_records}{\num{63727}} records, the record being the wrong unit here exactly as in the
experiments the contract governs. \artv{protected_objects}{Six} protected objects and \artv{state_channels}{six} state channels are
operational enumerations, not completeness claims.

\emph{Conclusion.} Chronological separation remains necessary, but it cannot establish validity
properties whose counterexamples live outside one realised dataset. Orbit-Evidence therefore changes the
object of validation: instead of more row-level checks, it encodes relational invariants across
executions and aggregation levels. Differential provenance makes an omitted
dependency falsifiable without enumerating the candidates; the unit gate asks whether dependence
survives aggregation and returns \textsc{indeterminate} when the design cannot decide. The unit gate fires on
real orbital structure, and the contract containing both operates unchanged on a frozen
third-party artifact, where a pre-registered correction changed model selection. Without such checks these obligations stay in prose, where a
reader cannot tell an argued assumption from a tested one---and satellite software is where we found
them because it separates the clock a state describes from the clock a record is stamped with, and
both from the publisher that decides when either becomes visible. What the evidence establishes is not completeness or improved accuracy,
but that assumptions normally asserted can be made executable, falsifiable, and able to refuse an
unsupported claim. The next step is broader independent replication across faults and pipelines
authored outside this work.
