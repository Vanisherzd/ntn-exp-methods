\title{Orbit-Evidence: Relational Validity Checks for\\
Learning-Assisted Satellite Communication Experiments}

\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Anonymous Submission}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Chronological train/validation/test splitting constrains temporal order but does not establish every
condition a valid deployment claim requires: some are relations across executions or aggregation
levels, not properties of a single run. We introduce
\emph{Orbit-Evidence}, an executable validity framework for learning-assisted satellite
communication experiments; it audits experiments of that class rather than proposing or training a
new learning model. Its two relational checks make provenance incompleteness
differentially falsifiable and test within-group dependence at the declared coarser grouping,
against a permutation reference admitting a third outcome, \textsc{indeterminate}, when the design
cannot decide. Satellite software supplies these obligations operationally, from
element-set epoch versus catalogue creation time to pass-level dependence. At one registered synthetic design the unit check's
false-halt rate is $\artv{l47_rate}{0.031}$, Wilson
$\artv{l47_wilson}{[0.018,0.052]}$, consistent with a nominal $\artv{l47_alpha}{0.05}$ rather than
better, while power is only $\artv{l47_power_02}{0.25}$ at $\rho=0.2$. Applied unchanged to real
catalogue records, pooled fit-update analyses over \artv{real_objects}{\num{11}} objects reject
exchangeability at all three tested groupings, whereas only \artv{real_halt}{one} of
\artv{real_objects}{\num{11}} per-object tests halts; the statistic is a clustering test statistic,
not a variance share, and no grouping is claimed correct. On a separately frozen
third-party spacecraft-anomaly \textsc{lstm} artifact the unmodified contract returns
\artv{ext_halt}{three} violations and \artv{ext_indet}{one} indeterminate condition; correcting one
pre-registered violation changes the selected model in all \artv{cons_seeds}{five} paired runs, the
downstream endpoint not being estimable at that resolution. A curated synthetic regression suite of
\artv{fault_class_count}{seventeen} represented validity-fault classes supplies regression coverage
of the represented violations, and \artv{red_fixture}{sixteen} of \artv{rule_count}{nineteen} rules have a
demonstrated red fixture. We claim no completeness, no radio-frequency result, and no
improvement in learned-method accuracy.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}
\input{figures/fig_architecture.tex}

Learning-assisted satellite experiments combine time-varying physical state, model- or data-driven
processing, and a decision evaluated later; correcting a propagated orbital prediction, adapting a
link parameter, or gating a learned branch against a model-based default are examples of the
class~\cite{lin2021ntn}. This paper addresses the validity of that experimental pipeline, not the
design of the learned component. Because such systems are temporal, evaluation is
organised as a chronological split---train on the past, validate on the near future, test on
the far future. The conditions under which ordered splitting is valid are
well established~\cite{bergmeir2012crossval}.

Our claim is that it is not sufficient, and that the gap is one of kind rather than degree. A
chronological split constrains the \emph{order} of quantities already in a dataset, and is silent
on four further questions. \emph{Availability}: a quantity may be past-dated and still
unpublished, so no system could have held it. \emph{Membership}: which rows exist can itself be
decided by later data, in both directions, and a split cannot examine rows never created.
\emph{Hidden state}: a split governs rows, whereas information travels through a feature tensor,
a scaler, a coefficient vector, a tracker's latent state, selection metadata, or an admission
bit---none of which is a column. \emph{Statistical units}: a split says nothing about whether the unit over
which uncertainty is computed is exchangeable.

These four share a structure, and the structure matters more than the list. A chronological split
is a \emph{predicate over the rows of one realised dataset}: given the table, its truth is
decidable. The four questions are not: each depends on something the realised run does
not contain---another source state, a registry rebuilt from a truncated source, a second execution,
or a coarser aggregation level---so each has a counterexample that exists only outside the dataset
in hand. We therefore distinguish
\emph{row-local} validity, evaluable from one realised run, from \emph{relational} validity, whose
truth depends on a relation between two executions, two source states, or two aggregation levels.
This distinction is the paper's foundation: splitting cannot, by construction, establish a property
whose counterexample lives outside the dataset in hand.

Three obvious repairs do not close the gap. A \emph{stricter split} still leaves past-dated but
unpublished information past-dated, cannot examine rows never created, and does not represent model
state as rows. \emph{More single-run assertions} cannot falsify a relation whose counterexample
requires a second execution. And \emph{richer provenance logging} cannot establish its own
completeness. The gap is structural, so the response has to change what is checked rather than how
much.

\subsection{Why the satellite domain makes this concrete}
\label{sec:whysat}

A catalogue provider periodically publishes an estimate of a satellite's orbital state; one such
record is an \emph{element set}, whose \emph{epoch} is the instant the state describes---distinct
from when the record was created or could be retrieved. \emph{Propagating} one element set forward
predicts future positions, hence the ground-station visibility intervals, or \emph{passes}, an
experiment schedules against. One element set therefore generates many passes, and a pass is one
\emph{row} of the experiment's table. Each inherits that one estimate's error: displace the parent
and its passes move together, so counting them as independent replicates can understate
uncertainty.

Satellite software supplies each obligation operationally. \emph{(i) Two clocks}: the creation stamp
\texttt{CREATION\_DATE} is exposed per element set by the public
catalogue~\cite{ccsds502omm,spacetrackgp}---an optimistic \emph{lower bound} on retrievability, not
the retrieval instant. It trails the epoch by an object-level median of
\qty{\artv{lag_object_median_h}{6.36}}{\hour} with a tail to days, so a feature formed from two
epochs passes every ordering test and is still unavailable if either was published after the
decision instant. The direction is not even guaranteed: we observed epoch $\le$ publication fail for
\artv{timing_outlier_objects}{one} of \artv{lag_objects}{\num{11}} objects (\S\ref{sec:threats}).
\emph{(ii) Row membership is generated by physics}: an opportunity exists only where the geometry is
predicted above an elevation mask from \emph{held} state, so a uniform grid filtered afterwards yields
rows no scheduler would emit. \emph{(iii) Labels arrive retrospectively}, and whether and when one
appears is the publisher's decision. \emph{(iv) Repeated measures nest}: the pass is too fine a
unit whenever the observable carries that inherited fit signature---\S\ref{sec:external}
measures this, and finds the element set too fine as well. \emph{(v) State survives
a nominal freeze}, stateful trackers refreshing at their own events, which can fall after a model is
declared frozen.

We therefore treat experimental validity as an \emph{executable contract}: each rule protects a
named object, states a violation, and is enforced by a check naming the rule it broke.

\textbf{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Two relational validity checks.} \emph{Differential provenance completeness}
(\rulename{L4.6}): rather than enumerating the dependencies a declared \emph{provenance manifest}
might omit, we test a
necessary relation---if behaviour changes, declared provenance must change---so two runs differing
in output under an identical manifest hash constitute a counterexample. Failure proves
incompleteness; success does not prove completeness, and that asymmetry is what makes the property
falsifiable without enumerating the input space. \emph{Statistical-unit adequacy}
(\rulename{L4.7}): choosing an aggregation level does not establish that it is exchangeable, so we
test whether dependence survives one declared physical level coarser against a permutation
reference, and \emph{abstain} when the realised geometry cannot attain the nominal level.
Insufficient resolution is not evidence of validity.
\item \textbf{A satellite experiment contract that instantiates them.} The two checks sit among
nineteen executable rules in four layers (Fig.~\ref{fig:contract},
Table~\ref{tab:rules}) that map the two clocks, row membership, label closure, persistent state and the
statistical unit onto the objects a deployment claim depends on. The catalogue is the
instantiation, not the novelty: three further rules are relational recastings of practice we cite,
and the remainder are row-local obligations that make the relational ones checkable at all.
Implemented as the \artv{source_loc}{\num{833}}-line \texttt{numpy}-only Orbit-Evidence toolkit.
\item \textbf{Evidence at three complementary levels.} \emph{Controlled regression fixtures}
establish rule reachability and the unit gate's operating characteristic
(Fig.~\ref{fig:opcurve}); \emph{real orbital catalogue records} for
\artv{real_objects}{\num{11}} objects test whether the unit problem arises in real data under the
declared observable and hierarchy; and \emph{a separately frozen public spacecraft-anomaly
\textsc{lstm} artifact} tests whether the unmodified contract operates outside the project that
built it, where \artv{ext_halt}{three} rules halt and
\artv{ext_nobs}{\num{5}} of \artv{ext_classified}{\num{19}} are recorded \emph{not observable}
rather than passed. A pre-registered correction of one detected violation changes model selection in
all five paired runs; the downstream endpoint is not estimable at the available paired-run
resolution.
\end{enumerate}

We claim nothing about radio-frequency or packet-level performance.

\emph{Availability.} The toolkit, fixtures, fault suite and summary artifact accompany this
submission anonymously. \texttt{make gate} regenerates the evidence and refuses to build if a headline number
\emph{disagrees} with the artifact at the sentence claiming it, if that sentence is missing, or if a
retracted claim reappears---a regression guard, not a proof of correctness. The two studies of
\S\ref{sec:external} are hash-verified, their inputs not distributable anonymously.

\subsection{The decision boundary and the protected objects}

Let $t_d$ be the \emph{decision instant}: the moment the declared deployment action would have to be
taken using only state already held; in a satellite-control instantiation this could be the instant
a transmission or scheduling action is committed. A deployment claim is interpretable only if every quantity entering the decision was obtainable
before $t_d$; Fig.~\ref{fig:contract} places the protected objects against the layers that check
them.

Six objects follow. \emph{Feature availability} must be computable at $t_d$, and the
\emph{availability clock} selects it on the message-creation bound, not the descriptive epoch. \emph{Row membership} may have a row's label status, value, uncertainty or
closure time changed by later information, but never its existence, and violations run both ways.
\emph{Label closure} makes a retrospective label usable only from the instant $t_c$ at which it
becomes final; use before it is one of the
two ordering violations a split does catch, while the harder failure is when the \emph{probability}
that a label exists depends on the covariate under study. \emph{State channels} carry information
that is not a column---feature tensor, scaler, coefficients, tracker state, selected-model
metadata~\cite{cawley2010selection}, the gate bit---an operational enumeration, not a completeness
claim. And the \emph{statistical unit} must be a level at which replicates of one physical event are
exchangeable, which aggregating somewhere does not establish.

\subsection{Four layers}

Fig.~\ref{fig:contract} places the layers against the experiment they observe and
Table~\ref{tab:rules} gives their targets and decision semantics. Two commitments make the
catalogue usable. The protected objects do not partition the rules: six enforce one
directly---the four \rulename{L1} rules \rulename{L1.1}--\rulename{L1.4}, among them the
availability clock (\rulename{L1.2}), plus \rulename{L3.1} and \rulename{L4.7}---and the rest make
those obligations checkable at all. And a rule is trusted only once demonstrated to fail on a
deliberately broken fixture.

\subsection{Primitives, prior art, and what is method}

We propose none of the primitives: $\mathrm{ICC}(1)$~\cite{shrout1979icc}, permutation inference
over exchangeability blocks~\cite{winkler2014permutation}, metamorphic relations over
runs~\cite{chen2018metamorphic}, hermetic dependency closure~\cite{mokhov2018buildsystems},
mutation testing~\cite{jia2011mutation}, hashing, and point-in-time correctness in feature stores.
Nor is the territory: production-readiness
rubrics~\cite{breck2017mltestscore}, hidden-state leakage~\cite{sculley2015debt},
pseudoreplication~\cite{hurlbert1984pseudoreplication}, leakage taxonomies~\cite{kaufman2012leakage}
and prevalence surveys~\cite{kapoor2023leakage}, and results on when ordered splitting is
valid~\cite{bergmeir2012crossval} all predate us. Element-set conventions are
standard~\cite{vallado2006revisiting}.

The method is the \emph{conversion}, and it is two transfers. Hermetic builds test dependency
closure over a build; we transfer that obligation to experiment provenance, where tracking systems
record declared parameters without testing whether the declaration is behaviourally complete. A
permutation reference is ordinarily an analysis step; we use it to calibrate a \emph{gate}, with an
operating characteristic and an abstention state. Generalised: identify which obligations are
relational, choose the counterfactual execution or coarser level that makes each falsifiable, encode
the invariant as a contract, and let an undecidable case return \textsc{indeterminate} rather than
pass. One debt: selection on a validation window is itself a leakage
channel~\cite{cawley2010selection}, hence selected-model metadata is a probed channel.

\section{Orbit-Evidence Toolkit}



\subsection{The two relational checks, formally}

One judgement generates both. Neither obligation can be \emph{certified}---enumerating the
dependencies a manifest might omit is hopeless, and no finite design proves exchangeability---so we
accept checks that can only \emph{refuse}, testing a necessary consequence instead. A failure then carries the weight---a counterexample for \rulename{L4.6}, a rejection at the
nominal level for \rulename{L4.7}---and a success carries none; the same stance forbids collapsing
an undecidable design into \textsc{pass}.

\emph{Provenance completeness} (\rulename{L4.6}). Over input variations $\{c_i\}$ with output
digests $o_i$ and manifest hashes $h_i$, any pair with $o_i \neq o_j$ and $h_i = h_j$ proves the
manifest incomplete, since two runs differing in output must differ in provenance. The converse is
redundant rather than erroneous, and that one-sided implication is the whole mechanism.

\emph{Statistical-unit adequacy} (\rulename{L4.7}). Given values $y$, unit identifiers $u$ and a
declared coarser grouping $g$, we aggregate to $\bar{y}_u$ and measure its intraclass correlation
\emph{within} $g$: within-group correlation there means the chosen unit shares state with its
neighbours, so intervals over it are optimistic. The ICC is used here as a \emph{clustering test
statistic}, not as a hierarchical variance decomposition; the permutation gate asks whether the
aggregated unit values are exchangeable across the fixed declared grouping.

The estimator is the one-way $\mathrm{ICC}(1)$ from variance
components~\cite{shrout1979icc}, whose numerator subtracts the within-group mean square and so has
expectation $0$ under independence, reported truncated at zero, and the
decision is referenced to a \emph{permutation null}: the aggregated unit values are permuted across
coarser groups---the exchangeability hypothesis under test---giving
$p=(1+\#\{\hat{\rho}^{*}\ge\hat{\rho}\})/(B+1)$ over $B=\num{400}$ permutations, halting at
$p\le\alpha=0.05$. The $+1$ is what makes the test valid: without it the empirical $1-\alpha$
quantile is anticonservative by $1/(B+1)$. Under that null a raw scatter ratio is a monotone
transform of $\hat{\rho}$ and gives the same $p$. We calibrate the operating characteristic
directly: over
\artv{l47_clean_paths}{\num{450}} clean evaluations it halts \artv{l47_false_halts}{\num{14}}
times, $\artv{l47_rate}{0.031}$, Wilson~\cite{wilson1927}
$\artv{l47_wilson}{[0.018,0.052]}$, rounded outward---consistent with the nominal $\artv{l47_alpha}{0.05}$, not
better---and detects the injected fault \artv{l47_injected}{\num{150}} of \artv{l47_injected}{\num{150}} times across the whole injected sweep, of which each point of
Fig.~\ref{fig:opcurve} is one design. All \artv{l47_clean_paths}{\num{450}} replicates sit
at one design whose clean fixture satisfies the null by construction, so this measures the
\emph{implementation}, and the interval rather than the point is the claim. Finally the rule \emph{declines to judge} below four coarser groups of two or more units, or
eight units in all. That floor is an \emph{attainability} precondition, not a power one: three groups of two
admit only $6!/(2!^{3}3!)=15$ distinct assignments, so an exact reference reaches no lower than
$1/15=0.067>\alpha$ and the rule abstains there rather than sample it, whereas four groups of two
admit $105$ and reach $1/105=0.0095$. Those are the designs' \emph{exact} resolutions; the rule never
enumerates them, always drawing the same $B$. The rule is therefore three-valued:
\textsc{halt}, the condition is violated; \textsc{indeterminate}, the design cannot support the
decision; and \textsc{pass}, which means \emph{not rejected}, not verified---at power $\artv{l47_power_02}{0.25}$ for
$\rho=0.2$ three genuinely dependent designs in four also pass. Collapsing \textsc{indeterminate}
into \textsc{pass} is how a design that could not have rejected comes to look like one that did not. No condition in our own suite reaches
the floor; the third-party artifact of \S\ref{sec:external} does.

\emph{Canaries and seeds.} Canary effectiveness (\rulename{L3.2}) separates a \emph{guarded} channel from an \emph{effective} probe, since an inert mutation reads as an undetectable leak. Seeds live in three disjoint namespaces and an inspected seed is burned.

\section{Evidence From Outside This Project}
\label{sec:external}

Everything to this point was produced here; the records and the artifact below were not. Table~\ref{tab:external} collects it.

\emph{Does the unit problem exist in real satellite data?} Yes. For each pass we take two
consecutive published element sets, propagate both to the pass midpoint, and measure how far the
predicted position moves \emph{along-track}---along the direction of travel---so this \emph{update
increment} quantifies how much a later catalogue update moved the earlier prediction, not error
against truth. If passes generated from one
element set move together under such updates, their pass-level observations are not exchangeable
under that declared grouping. Applying the frozen
\rulename{L4.7} rule to real Space-Track records, pass rows carrying this increment,
grouped by their generating element set, yield $\hat{\rho}=\artv{at_icc_d1}{0.501}$, permutation
$p=1/(B+1)\approx\artv{at_p}{0.0025}$ (no exceedance), and the rule
\textsc{halts}. Grouping this increment by object gives $\artv{at_icc_d2}{0.241}$; element-set means grouped by
object give $\artv{at_icc_d3}{0.284}$. Both also halt, all three at that same Monte-Carlo
floor, so no level tested here is exchangeable. Because passes sharing an element set also share
an object, this one-way statistic does not isolate an element-set-specific variance component:
the \textsc{halt} establishes non-exchangeability under the declared grouping, not a variance
decomposition across orbital levels.

The analysis covers \artv{real_objects}{\num{11}} objects, \artv{real_elsets}{\num{109}} element
sets and \artv{real_passes}{\num{331}} visible passes at one declared \textsc{wgs}-84 ground station
(\ang{\artv{st_lat}{24.7961}}\,N, \ang{\artv{st_lon}{120.9967}}\,E, \ang{\artv{st_mask}{10}} mask),
sampled at \qty{\artv{st_dt}{30}}{\second} over \qty{\artv{st_horizon}{24}}{\hour} from each element
set's epoch, \textsc{sgp4} supplying geometry only. Unit
counts are \artv{real_units_a}{\num{101}} and \artv{real_units_b}{\num{305}} rather than
\artv{real_elsets}{\num{109}} and \artv{real_passes}{\num{331}}: a record republished under a new
identifier keeps its epoch, so it and its passes collapse onto units already present. Of the \artv{real_passes}{\num{331}} passes,
\artv{at_dropped}{\num{59}} are dropped for having no successor element set, leaving
\artv{at_passes}{\num{272}} over \artv{at_elsets}{\num{90}} element sets for the along-track
analysis.

Two limits bound this. Because the increment differences two estimates rather than measuring error,
its median is only \qty{\artv{at_median_km}{0.226}}{\kilo\metre}; its unscreened tail reaches \qty{\artv{at_max_km}{559.022}}{\kilo\metre}. The rule adjudicates whether a declared level is adequate,
never which is right.

\emph{The observable determines what the rule can say.} Grouping element sets by object on
publication lag, and passes by element set on mean elevation, both passed at
$\hat{\rho}=\artv{real_icc_ab}{0.000},\ p=\artv{real_p_ab}{1.000}$ against our registered halts---a ties signature, the estimator
truncating at zero, so not an effect size. Mean elevation is near-invariant to which element set
propagated a pass and publication lag is a catalogue property, so neither carries the fit-update
signature. \artv{real_halt}{One} of eleven per-object tests halted and
\artv{real_pass}{10} passed, but $P(\ge 1)$ under the global null across those
\artv{at_decisions}{fourteen} decisions---eleven per object and three pooled---is
$\artv{at_p_any}{0.512}$.

\emph{Can the frozen contract operate outside the project that built it?} Yes. On a public
spacecraft-telemetry \textsc{lstm} artifact~\cite{hundman2018telemanom} frozen at a commit chosen
before any inspection, the unchanged contract returns the outcomes of
Table~\ref{tab:external}. A \emph{rule verdict} is
three-valued and is what a rule returns once its obligation applies; auditing adds two
\emph{dispositions} preceding any verdict---\emph{not applicable}, the obligation does not arise,
and \emph{not observable}, it arises but the inspected artifact does not expose the inputs the rule
needs. The second is never scored as compliance. Selection criteria were fixed
before searching and no detector was touched: the contract's \texttt{sha256} is identical before
and after, and equal to the pre-registration hash. The telemetry arrays came from a checksum-verified mirror
after the upstream endpoint became unavailable, per-file hashes matching two independently published
sources---mirror concordance, not verification by the original publisher.

The halt with methodological weight is validation-fold chronology
(\rulename{L4.1}): overlapping windows sharing \artv{cons_windows}{\num{259}} of
\artv{cons_span}{\num{260}} samples are shuffled and then split by fraction, so the early-stopping validation set is
drawn from the training window's own time span. We scope it to that signal rather than to the
reported test results. The other two are provenance
obligations research code is not generally written to satisfy---a declared configuration value the
implementation may silently reduce, and no hash binding a run to its inputs. Where provenance is incomplete we record
that the artifact does not expose enough, never that its authors erred.

\rulename{L4.7} also ran on the artifact's per-channel errors and \emph{abstained} at the grouping
its authors declare---two spacecraft, below its floor---then decided at a finer grouping the
artifact exposes: the abstention semantics behaving as specified on data nobody here produced.

\emph{Does a detected violation matter?} We pre-registered one intervention, froze the detector's
hash, then corrected only the partition. Carving the validation block chronologically and dropping
the \num{259} windows that make source-timestep overlap impossible takes overlap from
\qty{\artv{cons_overlap_pct}{100}}{\percent} of the validation support to zero and \rulename{L4.1}
from halt to pass. Across \artv{cons_seeds}{\num{5}} paired seeds it changed which model early
stopping selected \artv{cons_sel_changed}{\num{5}} times out of five, every checkpoint differing;
re-running one seed under identical conditions reproduces its checkpoint bit for bit, so this is the
intervention and not training noise. The reported detection metric moved on three seeds in
\emph{both} directions: an anomaly was flagged in \artv{cons_det_orig}{3} of five seeds before the
correction and \artv{cons_det_corr}{4} after, one seed losing a detection and two gaining one. We do not call that a null: with five paired seeds the smallest attainable
two-sided $p$ is $\artv{cons_minp}{0.0625}$, above $\alpha$, so by the same attainability argument
that makes \rulename{L4.7} abstain, this endpoint is \emph{not estimable at the available paired-run
resolution}---a disposition of this study's evidence, not a rule verdict---and the channel compounds
it, its target taking one value in training and two in test. We claim nothing about the upstream paper's
validity or about improving anomaly detection.

Two retrospective case studies from the stopped programme are in the artifact; both predate the
contract.

\section{Threats to Validity, Limitations and Conclusion}
\label{sec:threats}

\emph{Evaluation scope.} The curated fault suite is primarily regression evidence. Faults and rules largely share an
origin---one programme, mostly one hand---so $17/17$ measures detector
\emph{reachability}~\cite{jia2011mutation}, and hand-authored mutants overstate suite
strength~\cite{just2014mutants}. Most mutated objects are consumed only by another check, never
by a computation producing a reported number---the sharpest limit here; physical scale (\rulename{L2.2}), bounded divergence over the
evaluation window (\rulename{L2.3}) and provenance hashes (\rulename{L4.5}) fire in no curated
condition, though \rulename{L4.5} did halt on the third-party artifact. Only one fault was specified
before any detector existed. We therefore make no unseen-fault sensitivity claim: represented
violations, once fixed, do not silently return in the form the suite injects. The third-party study partially breaks the loop but remains one artifact and one channel. The
\artv{at_passes}{\num{272}}-pass along-track subset is conditional on a successor catalogue record
existing and is therefore not a random sample of the \artv{real_passes}{\num{331}}-pass cohort:
catalogue-update regimes with fewer or differently timed successor records may be
underrepresented, and we make no cohort-wide prevalence claim from this observable.

\emph{Contract scope.} Several rules give weaker guarantees than their names suggest, deliberately.
\rulename{L4.6} is bounded by the caller's variation set, so the input nobody thought of is absent
from the sweep by construction. Repeated measures (\rulename{L4.3}), a warning rule distinct from
\rulename{L4.7}, is a heuristic guard on a self-reported independence flag: its fixed
within-group-correlation threshold, uncalibrated unlike \rulename{L4.7}'s, halts about one correct
design in six at eight coarser groups. \rulename{L4.5} checks
that a manifest and self-hash exist, not that either is correct. \rulename{L4.7} adjudicates only
the grouping supplied to it; a within-object permutation would test element-set structure more
specifically, a stronger question than the present claim. It presumes a \emph{unique} next coarser
level that orbital data does not supply, since element sets and deployment episodes cross-cut. Four obligations remain outside the contract: $t_d$ is declared rather than
checked; covariate-coupled label availability has no rule, found by an \emph{ad hoc} diagnostic and
never promoted; exact retrieval time is unavailable; and common-mode effects are unmodelled, drag
driven by solar and geomagnetic activity being shared across every object in an interval. Epoch~$\le$~publication is likewise
sometimes false, in \artv{timing_outlier_objects}{one} of \artv{lag_objects}{\num{11}} objects over
its full history---reported per object rather than pooled over
\artv{lag_records}{\num{63727}} records, the record being the wrong unit here. \artv{protected_objects}{Six} protected objects and \artv{state_channels}{six} state channels are
operational enumerations, not completeness claims.

\emph{Conclusion.} Chronological separation remains necessary but cannot establish properties whose
counterexamples live outside one realised dataset. Orbit-Evidence therefore changes the object of
validation, encoding relational invariants across executions and aggregation levels. Differential provenance makes an omitted dependency falsifiable
without enumerating candidates; the unit gate asks whether dependence survives aggregation and
abstains when the design cannot decide. Both fire: on real orbital structure, and unchanged on a
frozen third-party artifact where a pre-registered correction changed model selection. What the evidence establishes is not completeness or improved accuracy,
but that assumptions normally asserted can be made executable, falsifiable, and able to refuse an
unsupported claim. The next step is broader independent replication across faults and pipelines
authored outside this work.
