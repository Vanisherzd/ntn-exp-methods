% ── Abstract ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\begin{abstract}
Direct-to-Satellite (D2S) IoT terminals must pre-compensate large LEO Doppler
shifts before every LR-FHSS uplink from an on-board orbital prediction that is, in
practice, a \emph{stale} TLE propagated open-loop by SGP4. The primary
contribution is a real-data negative finding: on current BLACK~KITE TLE
history, residual learning is not justified. We study the
pre-transmission endpoint decision: when should a learned inter-TLE residual be
trusted to modify the physics baseline, and when should the terminal keep
open-loop compensation? On real Space-Track TLE history for two BLACK~KITE LEO
satellites (NORAD~66741, 68474), with strict chronological leakage-free splits, a
learned inter-TLE residual does \emph{not} beat SGP4 at any tested staleness (8--168~h),
target-specific or cross-satellite. The proposed \emph{Evidence Gate} therefore
enables a learned corrector only when a chronological held-out window beats the
baseline by a margin~$\gamma$; otherwise it falls back to SGP4. On the real data
the gate \emph{closes}, so deployment is equivalent to never learning. A
controlled synthetic stress test illustrates the
complementary mechanism: the gate opens only when a dominant systematic residual
exists. Software-only proxies illustrate how timing/frequency uncertainty affects
guard and energy budgets. All ``reference'' Doppler is model-derived (SGP4 from
real TLE), not measured; no packet, link-layer, over-the-air, or live-satellite
result is claimed.
\end{abstract}

% ── Introduction ─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:intro}

Direct-to-Satellite (D2S) IoT using Low-Earth Orbit (LEO) satellites enables
connectivity for power-constrained end-devices in remote areas, but the high
relative velocity (several~km/s in LEO) induces Doppler shifts of tens of
kHz---order $20$~kHz at 868~MHz and roughly $50$~kHz at S-band, depending on
carrier frequency and pass geometry---together with rapid Doppler-rate changes
that stress the physical layer~\cite{hurn2023lrfhss}. Long-Range
Frequency-Hopping Spread Spectrum (LR-FHSS), characterized in Semtech's
AN1200.64 note~\cite{semtech2023lrfhss}, achieves robust uplink by distributing
transmit energy across a grid of narrow frequency bins, providing frequency
diversity against Doppler uncertainty. Because the sub-channels are narrow,
residual frequency error after pre-compensation is costly: it widens the guard
the terminal must reserve, increases the chance of missing a hop bin, and spends
airtime and energy that a battery-powered, unattended node cannot recover.

The terminal must size these margins \emph{before} transmitting, using only the
orbital information it holds on board---in practice a \emph{stale} TLE propagated
open-loop by SGP4/SDP4~\cite{hoots1980models}. This is the deployable
physics baseline. We transform classical PHY-layer Doppler compensation into
timing- and frequency-uncertainty-aware endpoint control for LR-FHSS D2S IoT under
stale orbital information and low-power constraints: prediction uncertainty is not
merely an accuracy statistic but the quantity that sizes the transmit-window guard,
the hop-bin frequency margin, and the energy spent per successful burst. A tempting
hypothesis is that a learned model can predict the model-derived inter-TLE
residual between a stale-TLE prediction and a later-TLE reference,
shrinking the error. The central
question of this paper is therefore not how to recover a waveform once it
exists, but a transmitter-side decision: \emph{when is it justified to learn a
correction on top of the physics prediction, and when is the safe physics
default the right choice?}

We expose a gap in the naive ``always learn'' answer: on real TLE history for two
BLACK~KITE satellites, a learned inter-TLE residual corrector does not beat the open-loop
SGP4 / stale-TLE baseline at any tested staleness---an always-on learner would, if
anything, \emph{increase} the frequency error it is meant to reduce. Learning
must therefore be \emph{earned} on held-out evidence, formalised as the
\emph{Evidence Gate}: a learned corrector deploys only when a chronological
held-out validation window shows it beats the physics baseline by a margin.

This paper is intentionally conservative: it does not argue that residual learning improves current BLACK~KITE operation. Instead, it asks whether a terminal should ever deploy such learning without local chronological evidence, and shows that for the available BLACK~KITE history the answer is no. The contribution is therefore not a stronger residual predictor, but a falsification-oriented endpoint policy: before a terminal spends guard, frequency margin, or energy on learned residual compensation, the learned branch must demonstrate chronological utility over the deployable SGP4 baseline. The BLACK~KITE result is useful precisely because residual learning can be plausible as a hypothesis yet still fail the deployment test.

\emph{Related work.} Prior art clusters into three layers, none of which contains
our decision problem. (i)~\emph{Link/capacity/energy} analyses quantify aggregate
LR-FHSS reliability and transmission cost under LEO Doppler and
load~\cite{semtech2023lrfhss,ullah2022lrfhss,hurn2023lrfhss}. (ii)~\emph{NTN synchronisation} estimates
timing advance and ToA/CFO, largely at the receiver or random-access
layer~\cite{3gpp2019tr38821}. (iii)~\emph{Orbit-learning} corrects
SGP4/SDP4~\cite{hoots1980models} trajectories---GP
correction~\cite{peng2019gp}, differentiable SGP4~\cite{acciarini2025dsgp4},
survey~\cite{caldas2024mlorbit}---targeting trajectory accuracy, while D2S
uplink policies~\cite{alvarez2022uplink} choose \emph{when} to transmit given a
prediction assumed trustworthy. None decides, at the endpoint and \emph{before}
transmission, whether a learned residual should be trusted under stale orbital
information, nor how timing/frequency uncertainty should size the LR-FHSS
guard, hop-bin margin, and energy policy.

Unlike receiver-side LR-FHSS studies and orbit-accuracy learning, we study the
\emph{pre-transmission endpoint decision}: is a learned residual
trustworthy enough to size timing/frequency/energy margins, or should the
terminal retain the SGP4 physics default? Contributions:
\begin{itemize}
\item \emph{Real-data negative finding:} on BLACK~KITE TLE history
(8--168~h, strict splits), learned inter-TLE residuals never beat SGP4;
always-learn is unsafe and the gate closes (Table~\ref{tab:bk},
Fig.~\ref{fig:gateev}).
\item \emph{Evidence-gated deploy/no-deploy rule:} a chronological
validation-window test rejects unsupported learning on real data and opens only
under controlled systematic synthetic evidence (Eqs.~\eqref{eq:gate}--\eqref{eq:deploy},
Tables~\ref{tab:gate}, \ref{tab:policy}); no distribution-shift guarantee is
claimed.
\item \emph{Endpoint-budget software proxy:}
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:guard}--\eqref{eq:esucc} connect timing/frequency uncertainty
to guard and energy proxies (Fig.~\ref{fig:proxies}); these are software-only
control proxies, not packet or link validation.
\end{itemize}

% ── System Model ──────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{System Model}
\label{sec:system}

\subsection{LEO D2S Channel Characteristics}
In a LEO pass the one-way Doppler reaches the tens-of-kHz magnitudes of
Sec.~\ref{sec:intro}, with the largest rate near closest approach. The Doppler
shift is modeled as
\begin{equation}
f_D(t) = -\frac{v_r(t)}{c}\,f_c
\label{eq:doppler}
\end{equation}
where $v_r(t)$ is the radial velocity (positive when satellite recedes) and $c$
is the speed of light. The residual CFO after pre-compensation is
\begin{equation}
f_{\mathrm{res}}(t) = f_D(t) - \hat{f}_D(t) + f_{\mathrm{osc}},
\label{eq:residual_cfo}
\end{equation}
where $\hat{f}_D$ is the predicted Doppler and $f_{\mathrm{osc}}$ is the terminal
oscillator offset. We model reliable hop reception as $|f_{\mathrm{res}}|$ staying
small relative to a fixed frequency tolerance $F_{\mathrm{tol}}$; this is a control
proxy, not a standard-compliant receiver model.

\subsection{Stale-TLE Open-Loop Baseline and the Learned Residual}
\label{sec:baseline}
The terminal holds a TLE that is, in general, older than the present epoch.
Propagating this \emph{stale} TLE with SGP4~\cite{hoots1980models} gives the
deployable open-loop Doppler prediction $f_{\mathrm{phys}}(t)$. To study whether
this can be improved, we define a model-derived \emph{reference} Doppler
$D_{\mathrm{ref}}(t)$ as the SGP4 propagation of a \emph{later} TLE for the same
satellite at the same UTC. We emphasise that
\texttt{reference\_is\_measured\_truth = false}: $D_{\mathrm{ref}}$ is a newer
TLE solution, not an RF measurement of satellite Doppler. This residual is not
over-the-air Doppler error; it is the residual observable to a TLE-only terminal
by comparing stale and later TLE propagations. The quantity a learner
would have to predict is the inter-TLE residual
\begin{equation}
r(t) = D_{\mathrm{ref}}(t) - f_{\mathrm{phys}}(t),
\label{eq:residual}
\end{equation}
which encodes mean-element updates, drag-term changes, and numerical fit
differences between successive TLE solutions. A learned corrector forms
$f_{\mathrm{ml}}(t)=f_{\mathrm{phys}}(t)+\hat r(x_t)$ from a feature vector $x_t$
(TLE age, epoch gap, stale Doppler, orbital phase, geometry, and stale orbital
elements). All Doppler is computed at 868~MHz for a representative ground station
(24$^\circ$N, 121$^\circ$E).

\subsection{The Evidence Gate}
\label{sec:gate}
Let $V$ be a chronological \emph{validation} window, strictly later than the
training span and strictly earlier than the held-out test span, so that no future
TLE leaks into model selection. On $V$ we measure the mean absolute frequency
error of the physics baseline and of the learned corrector against the
model-derived reference,
\begin{align}
\mathrm{MAE}_{\mathrm{phys}}(V) &= \tfrac{1}{|V|}\textstyle\sum_{t\in V}\big|D_{\mathrm{ref}}(t)-f_{\mathrm{phys}}(t)\big|,\\
\mathrm{MAE}_{\mathrm{ml}}(V)   &= \tfrac{1}{|V|}\textstyle\sum_{t\in V}\big|D_{\mathrm{ref}}(t)-f_{\mathrm{ml}}(t)\big|.
\end{align}
The Evidence Gate (Fig.~\ref{fig:architecture}) enables learning only on a proven margin,
\begin{equation}
G = \mathbf{1}\!\left[\,\mathrm{MAE}_{\mathrm{ml}}(V) < \gamma\,\mathrm{MAE}_{\mathrm{phys}}(V)\,\right],
\qquad \gamma\in(0,1],
\label{eq:gate}
\end{equation}
and the deployed corrector defaults to physics otherwise,
\begin{equation}
\hat f(t) = G\,f_{\mathrm{ml}}(t) + (1-G)\,f_{\mathrm{phys}}(t).
\label{eq:deploy}
\end{equation}

By construction $\mathrm{MAE}_{\hat f}(V)\le \mathrm{MAE}_{\mathrm{phys}}(V)$
\emph{on the validation window only}---not a distribution-shift bound. The gate is
a binary test with two error modes: a \emph{false open} deploys a noisy learner
with nothing to correct, a \emph{missed open} keeps the physics baseline and
forgoes a smaller margin; $\gamma$ is the conservatism knob. We use
$\gamma=0.95$ and report sensitivity across $\gamma$ and validation-window size
(Sec.~\ref{sec:eval-sens}), making no net-improvement claim; the gate re-checks
the learned branch on each fresh window and reverts to physics whenever the
evidence lapses. MAE is used as the gate loss because it is stable on small
chronological windows; tail/outage proxies are reported after gating and do not
override the deploy/no-deploy decision.
\label{sec:stat}

\subsection{Control Proxies}
\label{sec:proxies}
The deployed error $e(t)=D_{\mathrm{ref}}(t)-\hat f(t)$ sets the frequency margin
to reserve and the chance a hop falls outside its bin. Three proxies are used
across all experiments: the \emph{guard-band proxy} $g = 2\,p_{99}(|e|)$; the
\emph{outage proxy} $\rho = \Pr[\,|e| > F_{\mathrm{tol}}\,]$ with a representative
sub-kHz bin tolerance $F_{\mathrm{tol}}=500$~Hz; and the dimensionless
\emph{energy/overhead proxy}
\begin{equation}
E_{\mathrm{proxy}} \;\propto\; \Big(1 + \alpha_g\,\tfrac{g}{B}\Big)\,(1 + \rho),
\label{eq:eproxy}
\end{equation}
where $B$ is the hopping bandwidth and $\alpha_g\!\ge\!0$ weights idle guard
against retransmission pressure. All three are control-layer proxies for terminal
margin sizing, not packet-error, bit-error, or link-budget measurements; the
500~Hz tolerance is a representative sub-kHz hop-bin/control proxy, not a
standard receiver threshold. Timing
couples in through the Doppler rate, which peaks near closest approach: a
residual timing offset $\Delta t$ costs frequency margin
\begin{equation}
\Delta f_D(t) \;\approx\; \dot f_D(t)\,\Delta t,
\label{eq:ratecoupling}
\end{equation}
motivating pass-aware allocation; the reported guard $g$ remains a scalar
pass-aggregated $p_{99}$ statistic.

For the endpoint-budget studies (Sec.~\ref{sec:eval-endpoint}) the same
philosophy yields a \emph{coverage-proxy chain}, stated here exactly as
implemented in the released scripts. Model the residual timing offset and
residual frequency error as zero-mean Gaussians,
$\delta_t\!\sim\!\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_t^2)$ and
$f_{\mathrm{res}}\!\sim\!\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_f^2)$. The terminal reserves an
uncertainty-sized timing guard
\begin{equation}
g_t = g_0 + k\,\sigma_t, \qquad k=3,
\label{eq:guard}
\end{equation}
and the two miss proxies are the Gaussian tail coverages
\begin{equation}
P_t = \Pr[|\delta_t|>g_t] = \operatorname{erfc}\!\Big(\tfrac{g_t}{\sigma_t\sqrt{2}}\Big),
\quad
P_f = \operatorname{erfc}\!\Big(\tfrac{F_{\mathrm{tol}}}{\sigma_f\sqrt{2}}\Big).
\label{eq:miss}
\end{equation}
Treating the two mechanisms as independent gives the joint success proxy
\begin{equation}
S = (1-P_t)(1-P_f),
\label{eq:joint}
\end{equation}
and each attempt is charged the receiver-on guard/listen energy plus the
transmit energy, so the energy per successful burst is
\begin{equation}
E_{\mathrm{att}} = I_{\mathrm{rx}}V\,(g_t + t_{\mathrm{rx}}) + P_{\mathrm{tx}}\,t_{\mathrm{tx}},
\qquad
E_{\mathrm{succ}} = E_{\mathrm{att}}/S.
\label{eq:esucc}
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:guard} explains the Fig.~\ref{fig:proxies} shape: the
adaptive guard pins $P_t$ near the $k$-sigma floor
($\operatorname{erfc}(3/\sqrt{2})\!\approx\!0.3\%$) while $g_t$---and hence
$E_{\mathrm{att}}$---grows linearly with $\sigma_t$, so timing uncertainty is
paid in guard energy rather than misses. The timing-only sweep
(Fig.~\ref{fig:proxies}(a,b)) uses $S=1-P_t$; the ablation
(Fig.~\ref{fig:proxies}(c,d)) uses Eq.~\eqref{eq:joint}.

\subsection{Datasets and Splits}
\label{sec:data}
Real evaluation uses Space-Track TLE history for two BLACK~KITE LEO satellites:
BLACK~KITE-1 (NORAD~66741, 415 records, median inter-TLE gap $6.3$~h) and
BLACK~KITE-2 (NORAD~68474, 184 records, median gap $6.4$~h). For each reference
(newer) TLE, an older TLE inside a target staleness band is paired with it,
propagated open-loop, and compared to the reference at 24 UTC samples over one
orbital period; a pair is rejected if any sample has $|r|>1500$~Hz. Splits are
chronological by reference epoch (60/20/20 train/validation/test), and the
validation window uses only completed TLE-refresh intervals whose newer TLE
arrived before the next deployment decision, so the gate is causal. Compact CSV
summaries and regeneration commands are indexed in the artifact manifest. The
synthetic stress study (Sec.~\ref{sec:eval-stress}) is a controlled simulation,
no Space-Track data.

At each control epoch the terminal re-evaluates Eqs.~\eqref{eq:gate}--\eqref{eq:deploy}
on $V$ and re-sizes $g=2\,p_{99}(|e|)$---no receiver feedback, recomputed
whenever a fresh TLE arrives.

% ── Evaluation ────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────
\section{Evaluation}
\label{sec:eval}

\subsection{Experimental Setup}
\emph{Evaluation scope.} C1 is the real BLACK~KITE inter-TLE negative control;
C2 is the controlled synthetic gate-open mechanism check; C3 is the
software-only endpoint proxy study; C4 is artifact-level reproducibility for
scripts and compact summaries. C1--C3 are model-derived software evaluations,
not RF, packet, or over-the-air measurements.

\emph{Carrier convention.} Software Doppler and control metrics are reported at
an 868~MHz carrier (python-SGP4, ground station of Sec.~\ref{sec:baseline}),
matching common LR-FHSS D2S evaluation settings; the controller is
frequency-parametric (Eq.~\eqref{eq:doppler}) and residuals scale linearly with
$f_c$. Learned candidates are ridge regression, random forest, gradient
boosting, and a small MLP, selected on the chronological validation segment with
the gate evaluated once on the held-out test segment. A window is supported only
if the selected model beats the zero-residual baseline on held-out MAE by the
gate margin ($\gamma=0.95$) and also improves RMSE. All results in
Secs.~\ref{sec:eval-real}--\ref{sec:eval-endpoint} are software-only and
model-derived; no hardware, RF, UART, replay, or measured Doppler is involved.

\subsection{Real BLACK~KITE Negative Result}
\label{sec:eval-real}
Table~\ref{tab:bk} and Fig.~\ref{fig:gateev}(a,b) report the held-out test MAE of
the validation-selected learned model against the zero-residual stale-TLE
baseline: the learned inter-TLE residual is worse at \emph{every} tested
staleness, both target-specific (BK1, 8--168~h) and cross-satellite
(BK1$\rightarrow$BK2), so the gate of Eq.~\eqref{eq:gate} closes in all cases.
Thus, for the real BLACK~KITE rows, the deployed Evidence-Gate policy is exactly
the never-learn/SGP4 policy; its value is rejecting always-on learning.
Even at 168~h the BK1 inter-TLE baseline MAE is only 26.9~Hz---small against
$F_{\mathrm{tol}}=500$~Hz. This small residual scale is the finding: for BLACK
KITE there is little useful signal for always-on residual ML, so learning can
only add generalization noise. The negative result is therefore a warning
against deploying residual ML by default, not a failed improvement result.
This changes the role of learning in the endpoint stack. A residual learner
should not be treated as a default enhancement module; it is an untrusted
candidate whose deployment burden is higher than the SGP4 baseline. The gate
protects the terminal from converting validation noise into additional guard,
frequency margin, or retransmission pressure.

The result is consistent with how the data arises: successive Space-Track TLEs
reflect orbit-determination updates over different observation arcs, so the
inter-TLE residual mixes fit artifacts, drag mismatch, tracking noise, and
maneuver-like events---near zero-mean from the terminal-feature perspective, so
a regressor fits validation noise rather than a stable correction; richer
features or longer staleness could still expose learnable structure, precisely
the case the gate admits. Both $D_{\mathrm{ref}}$ and $f_{\mathrm{phys}}$ are
SGP4 propagations: a model-to-model inter-TLE residual, not a measured
RF-channel residual.

\subsection{Synthetic Stress: When the Gate Opens}
\label{sec:eval-stress}
A controlled synthetic simulation (no Space-Track data) generates the residual as
$r = A\,s(x) + \mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^2)$ with a fixed nonlinear systematic
$s(\cdot)$ and per-regime $(A,\sigma)$ bracketing gate behaviour; outcomes are
summarised in Table~\ref{tab:gate} and Fig.~\ref{fig:gateev}(c,d). The
synthetic run uses 20\,000 samples per regime and a chronological
12\,000/4\,000/4\,000 train/validation/test split. The real
BLACK~KITE experiment is thus a \emph{negative control} for unsafe learning:
when the residual lacks stable learnable structure, the gate correctly retains
SGP4. The synthetic systematic regime verifies the complementary case: when a
dominant residual structure exists, the same rule opens and reduces the
residual, guard, and outage proxies (Table~\ref{tab:gate}; overhead proxies, not
measured power savings). The gate is therefore a deploy/no-deploy test, not an
always-learn mechanism.

Two control-proxy studies instantiate Eqs.~\eqref{eq:guard}--\eqref{eq:esucc}

\emph{Timing uncertainty is paid as guard energy}

Table~\ref{tab:bk}; it is not a BLACK~KITE deployment outcome. On the real data,
the gate stays closed and the controller equals the baseline
(Sec.~\ref{sec:eval-real}).

\section{Conclusion and Limitations}
\label{sec:conclusion}

We recast PHY-layer Doppler compensation as timing- and
frequency-uncertainty-aware endpoint control for LR-FHSS D2S IoT under stale
orbital information: open-loop SGP4 is the default, and a learned residual is
deployed only when a chronological held-out window beats the baseline by a
margin. The strongest real result is negative: on BLACK~KITE TLE history the
learned correction never beats the baseline, so the gate closes and the terminal
keeps the conservative fallback. The controlled synthetic result is a mechanism
check showing that the same rule can open under dominant systematic evidence,
not a real-data improvement claim. The Evidence Gate is therefore most useful as an audit rule rather than as a guaranteed improvement mechanism: on BLACK~KITE it chooses the same action as never-learn, but this is precisely the safe action because it prevents an always-learn policy from increasing the residual. In a deployed terminal, learned residual branches should remain quarantined until they repeatedly re-earn deployment on fresh chronological evidence. For implementation, the terminal-side audit record should include the stale-TLE identifier, refresh time, validation interval, baseline MAE, learned MAE, $\gamma$, $G$, and the resulting guard/frequency policy. Such logs make failed passes diagnosable and prevent silent always-on ML deployment.

Practically, for BLACK~KITE-like TLE-only residuals the safe endpoint policy is
SGP4/never-learn unless held-out evidence proves otherwise. Reproducibility
artifacts index compact CSV summaries, regeneration scripts, frozen
tables/figures, and the compile-only NUCLEO footprint artifact; these artifacts
support auditability, not hardware or link validation. The next software
campaign is a multi-satellite generalization matrix with residual-learnability
analysis. This separates orbital uncertainty from other endpoint RF errors. Oscillator offset, receiver synchronization, and channel effects may dominate the absolute RF residual; our experiments isolate only the TLE-driven component observable from public orbital data. A practical stack should combine this gate with oscillator calibration and a tail-aware deployment loss.

\label{sec:limits}
All control results are software-only and model-derived
(\texttt{reference\_is\_measured\_truth=false}): no measured Doppler truth,
live-satellite contact, standards-compliant LR-FHSS decoding, PER/BER/CRC/PDR,
or gateway acknowledgement. The negative result holds for two
BLACK~KITE satellites and our feature set, not universally; the positive learning
result is synthetic only; the gate gives a validation-window property, not a
distribution-shift bound; the proxies of Sec.~\ref{sec:eval-endpoint} are control
proxies, not link-budget measurements. Tail-aware or cost-aware gates based on
p99 error, guard cost, or outage proxy are left for future work.
Packet-level conducted PER/PDR against a reference receiver and authorized
over-the-air measurement are future work, in that order.
