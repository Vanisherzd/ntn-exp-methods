\title{Orbit-Evidence: Relational Validity Checks for\\
Learning-Assisted Satellite Communication Experiments}

\author{\IEEEauthorblockN{Anonymous Submission}}

\maketitle

\begin{abstract}
Chronological train/validation/test splitting does not establish every property a deployment
claim needs, and some of those properties cannot be established from a single run at all: they
are relations \emph{between} executions. We formulate two such properties and make both
executable. \emph{Provenance completeness}: if two runs differ in output under an identical
declared provenance closure, the closure is incomplete---refutable differentially, never by
inspecting a manifest. \emph{Statistical-unit adequacy}: the chosen unit must be checked for
residual dependence at the next coarser physical level, which we decide against a permutation
reference with three outcomes rather than two---\textsc{pass}, \textsc{halt}, and
\textsc{indeterminate} when the design cannot support a decision at all. Satellite software supplies the obligations these sit among---an element set carries an epoch and a separate catalogue publication time, labels arrive when a catalogue publishes, passes from one element set are repeated measures---and Orbit-Evidence packages them as nineteen rules behind a CI gate. The unit rule is the one component whose error rates we measure---\num{14} false halts over \num{450} clean replicates, $0.031$ against a nominal $0.05$, power $1.00$ at intraclass correlation $0.8$ but $0.25$ at $0.2$---and it then leaves its fixture: unchanged, it decides on real catalogue records for eleven objects, and the unmodified nineteen rules classify one third-party artifact frozen before any rule ran, with five rules recorded as \emph{not observable} rather than passed. A curated seventeen-fault suite supplies regression coverage---not completeness, not sensitivity, and no radio-frequency or learned-method claim.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Learning components are increasingly proposed as assistants to physical models in satellite
and non-terrestrial communication software: correcting a propagated orbital prediction,
adapting a link parameter, or deciding whether a learned branch should override a model-based
default~\cite{lin2021ntn}. Because such systems are temporal, evaluation is
organised as a chronological split---train on the past, validate on the near future, test on
the far future. That is a common and necessary practice for temporal learning pipelines, and
the conditions under which ordered splitting is valid are well
established~\cite{bergmeir2012crossval}.

Our claim is that it is not sufficient, and that the gap is one of kind rather than degree. A
chronological split constrains the \emph{order} of quantities already in a dataset, and is silent
on four further questions. \emph{Availability}: a quantity may be past-dated and still
unpublished, so no system could have held it. \emph{Membership}: which rows exist can itself be
decided by later data, in both directions, and a split cannot examine rows never created.
\emph{Hidden state}: a split governs rows, whereas information travels through a feature tensor,
a scaler, a coefficient vector, a tracker's latent state, selection metadata, or an admission
bit---none of which is a column. \emph{Statistical units}: a split says nothing about whether the unit over
which uncertainty is computed is exchangeable.

\subsection{Why the satellite domain makes this concrete}
\label{sec:whysat}

These gaps are abstract in general and specific here. Six properties of satellite communication
software give each protected object an operational meaning, and are why we develop the contract in
this domain rather than as generic tooling.

\emph{(i) Two clocks.} An element set has an \emph{element epoch}---the instant its state vector
describes---and a separate \emph{publication time}, \texttt{CREATION\_DATE}, exposed per element
set by the public catalogue~\cite{ccsds502omm,spacetrackgp}; they typically differ by hours with a
tail to days. A feature formed from two element epochs is a difference of two past timestamps: it
passes every ordering test and is still unavailable if either was published after the decision
instant. \emph{(ii) The decision instant is a real endpoint}, using the state it \emph{holds}, not
the best that exists, so availability is first-class. \emph{(iii) Schedules live inside predicted
geometry}---a supporting physical obligation, not a seventh object: an opportunity exists only
where the link is predicted visible above a mask, so a uniform grid filtered afterwards yields rows
no scheduler would emit. \emph{(iv) Labels arrive retrospectively}, and whether and when one
appears is the publisher's decision. \emph{(v) Repeated measures nest}: every pass propagated from
one element set shares that set's largely along-track error, so the exchangeable unit is usually
the \emph{element set}, not the pass---\S\ref{sec:external} measures this. \emph{(vi) State
survives a nominal freeze}, stateful trackers refreshing at their own events, which can fall after
a model is declared frozen.

We therefore treat experimental validity as an \emph{executable contract}: each rule protects a
named object, states a violation, and is enforced by a check naming the rule it broke. Our thesis: Orbit-Evidence turns deployment-time
availability, row membership, model-state and statistical-unit assumptions into executable CI
checks for satellite communication experiments, and a curated regression suite demonstrates
those checks on seventeen known fault classes that chronological ordering alone is not
designed to detect.

\textbf{Contributions.}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Validity conditions that are not predicates on one table.} Most checks test values
inside a dataset; five of ours cannot be written that way, of which two are the contribution and
three are relational recastings of practice we cite. Four compare \emph{executions}:
recompute a feature with state after $t$ truncated and require bit-identity (\rulename{L1.1});
rebuild a registry from a truncated source under the same declared window (\rulename{L1.4});
require a probe mutation to change an observable before its non-detection means anything
(\rulename{L3.2}); and refute a provenance closure when two runs differ in output under an
identical manifest hash, the converse being merely redundant (\rulename{L4.6})---an asymmetry
that makes the property falsifiable without enumerating the input space. The fifth compares
\emph{aggregation levels}: \rulename{L4.7} tests whether the chosen unit stays exchangeable one
level coarser, with an explicit \textsc{indeterminate} outcome. Neither its estimator nor its
permutation test is new; treating the calibrated test, its operating characteristic and its
abstention state as a workflow gate is.
\item \textbf{A satellite experiment contract, and Orbit-Evidence.} Nineteen executable rules over
six protected objects in four layers (Table~\ref{tab:rules}), separating \emph{core} obligations
from \emph{support} rules that make the experiment auditable rather than following from deployment
causality, in a \artv{source_loc}{\num{833}}-line \texttt{numpy}-only package.
\item \textbf{Calibrated and externalised executable evidence.} \rulename{L4.7} carries a
measured false-halt rate and power curve with a deterministic abstention boundary
(Fig.~\ref{fig:opcurve}). It then leaves the fixture twice (Table~\ref{tab:rules}): the frozen
rule decides on real catalogue records for \artv{real_objects}{\num{11}} objects under three
declared groupings, and the \emph{unmodified} contract classifies all
\artv{ext_classified}{\num{19}} rules against one public third-party artifact frozen before any
rule ran---\artv{ext_halt}{three} halts with exact locations, and
\artv{ext_nobs}{\num{5}} rules recorded as \emph{not observable} rather than passed. Curated
regression coverage of seventeen fault classes (Table~\ref{tab:rules}) supports this as
regression evidence: the represented violations cannot silently return, which is not sensitivity
to faults the suite lacks (\S\ref{sec:threats}).
\end{enumerate}

We claim nothing about radio-frequency or packet-level performance. The object of study is the
experiment, not the model.

\emph{Availability.} The toolkit, fixtures, fault suite and summary artifact accompany this
submission anonymously. \texttt{make gate} regenerates the fault matrix and \rulename{L4.7}'s operating characteristic and
refuses to build if a headline number \emph{disagrees} with the artifact at the sentence claiming
it, if such a sentence is missing, or if a retracted claim reappears---agreement per claim, not
presence somewhere. The two studies of \S\ref{sec:external} it reads rather than reruns, their
inputs not shipping; it refuses if the contract's hash or the frozen third-party commit differ from
the registered ones. Weaker than regeneration, and we say so. It is a regression guard throughout,
not a proof of correctness.

\section{Threat Model and Experiment Contract}

\subsection{Decision time and the six protected objects}

Let $t_d$ be the \emph{decision instant}: the moment a deployed system must act using only
state it already holds---for an open-loop satellite endpoint, when a transmission is
committed from the element set on board. A deployment claim is interpretable only if every
quantity entering the decision was obtainable before $t_d$. Six objects are therefore
\emph{protected}; Fig.~\ref{fig:contract} shows their relationship in time.

Each object is the obligation one domain property of
\S\ref{sec:whysat} imposes; the parenthetical names which. \emph{Feature availability} and the
\emph{availability clock} (i): a feature must be computable from state available at $t_d$, selected
on publication rather than the descriptive clock. \emph{Row membership} (iv): later information may change a label's status, value,
uncertainty or closure time, never whether a row exists; violations run both ways, rows dropped
when a later reference fails to appear and rows created when a schedule's extent comes from the
data on hand. \emph{Label closure} (iv): a retrospective label is usable only from $t_c$, and use
before it is the one ordering violation a split does catch---dotted in Fig.~\ref{fig:contract}.
The harder failure is when the \emph{probability} that a label exists depends on the covariate
under study, which no split addresses. \emph{State channels} (vi): information also enters through
the feature tensor, scaler, coefficients, a tracker's latent state, selected-model
metadata~\cite{cawley2010selection} or the gate bit---the six probed channels, an operational
enumeration, not a completeness claim. \emph{Statistical units} (v): replicates of one physical
event are not independent observations, and aggregating somewhere does not establish that level is
exchangeable. Predicted-visible geometry (iii) is a supporting physical obligation, not a seventh
object.

\subsection{Four layers}

The six objects do not partition the nineteen rules, and we do not claim they do. Six protect one
directly---\rulename{L1.1}--\rulename{L1.4}, \rulename{L3.1}, \rulename{L4.7}---and the rest are
the layers those obligations need to be checkable at all (Table~\ref{tab:rules}): domain sanity,
the canary-effectiveness precondition without which an inert probe reads as an absent leak, and
reproducibility hygiene, which is independent of the causal model rather than derived from it.

Two design commitments make the contract usable. Every violation raises an exception carrying its
\emph{rule identifier}, so a finding names what it broke instead of surfacing a bare assertion
failure. And a detector is trusted only once demonstrated to fail on a deliberately broken
fixture: a check that cannot go red is not evidence.

\subsection{Relation to existing practice}

Most of what follows is not new in isolation, and we state the delta rather than the territory.
Executable production-readiness
rubrics~\cite{breck2017mltestscore,polyzotis2019datavalidation}, point-in-time correctness in
feature stores, hidden-state leakage as configuration coupling~\cite{sculley2015debt},
pseudoreplication~\cite{hurlbert1984pseudoreplication}, boundary-value analysis and leakage
taxonomies~\cite{kaufman2012leakage} with prevalence surveys~\cite{kapoor2023leakage} all predate
us; time-series work establishes when ordered splitting is valid~\cite{bergmeir2012crossval}, and
we address what it cannot express. Two neighbours matter most. \rulename{L4.6} is a metamorphic relation over runs~\cite{chen2018metamorphic}, and hermetic build systems already test whether declared inputs form a complete dependency closure~\cite{mokhov2018buildsystems}; we transfer that relational completeness obligation to experiment provenance, where tracking systems record declared parameters without testing whether the declaration is behaviourally complete. And permutation inference over
exchangeability blocks is standard~\cite{winkler2014permutation}, as is
$\mathrm{ICC}(1)$~\cite{shrout1979icc}: we propose neither a new estimator nor a new test. The
contribution is treating the calibrated test, its operating characteristic and its abstention
state as an executable validity gate. Selection on a validation window is itself a leakage
channel~\cite{cawley2010selection}, which is why selected-model metadata is one of the six
channels \rulename{L3.1} probes. Binding each number to the sentence claiming it is weaker than
literate computing~\cite{knuth1984literate}, which makes disagreement impossible rather than
detected; we detect because the manuscript and the evaluation are separate artifacts. Our evaluation is fault injection in the mutation-testing
tradition~\cite{jia2011mutation}; manifests follow the discipline urged for datasets~\cite{gebru2021datasheets}, and element conventions are standard~\cite{vallado2006revisiting}.

\section{Orbit-Evidence Toolkit}

\emph{Engineering.} Three components carry no argument of their own and are described in the
artifact: a visible-pass scheduler emitting only from intervals predicted by held state, a
freeze-then-label registry hashing rows before any label source is consulted, and a coverage-only
reference ensemble. All three enter the manifest.

\subsection{Two rules that inspect a relation between runs}

\emph{Provenance completeness} (\rulename{L4.6}) cannot be verified by examining a manifest, which
is silent about what it omits, but it is verifiable differentially. Over input variations
$\{c_i\}$ with output digests $o_i$ and manifest hashes $h_i$, any pair with $o_i \neq o_j$ and
$h_i = h_j$ proves the manifest incomplete: two runs differing in output must differ in
provenance. The converse is merely redundant, not an error. That asymmetry makes the rule
checkable without enumerating the input space.

\emph{Statistical unit selection} (\rulename{L4.7}) asks not whether replicates were aggregated but
whether the level they were aggregated \emph{to} is exchangeable. Given values $y$, unit
identifiers $u$ and a declared coarser grouping $g$, we aggregate to $\bar{y}_u$ and measure its
intraclass correlation \emph{within} $g$: residual correlation there means the chosen unit shares
state with its neighbours, so intervals over it are optimistic.

The estimator is the unbiased one-way $\mathrm{ICC}(1)$ from variance
components~\cite{shrout1979icc}, expectation $0$ under independence at every group size, and the
decision is referenced to a \emph{permutation null}: unit values are permuted across coarser
groups---the exchangeability hypothesis under test---giving
$p=(1+\#\{\hat{\rho}^{*}\ge\hat{\rho}\})/(B+1)$ over $B=\num{400}$ permutations, halting at
$p\le\alpha=0.05$. The $+1$ is not cosmetic: the empirical $1-\alpha$ quantile is anticonservative
by $1/(B+1)$, this form exact for any $B$. The reference distribution, not the estimator,
calibrates the decision---under that null a raw scatter ratio is a monotone transform of
$\hat{\rho}$ and gives the same $p$---while the estimator matters for the reported value and for
\rulename{L4.3}, which has no reference at all and at a fixed $0.2$ halts on about one correct
design in six. Specificity is a rate, so we measure it as one: over
\artv{l47_clean_paths}{\num{450}} clean evaluations it halts \artv{l47_false_halts}{\num{14}}
times, $\artv{l47_rate}{0.031}$, Wilson~\cite{wilson1927}
$\artv{l47_wilson}{[0.018,0.052]}$---consistent with the nominal $\artv{l47_alpha}{0.05}$, not
better---and detects the injected fault \num{150} of \num{150} times. All \num{450} replicates sit
at one design whose clean fixture satisfies the null by construction, so this measures the
\emph{implementation}, and the interval rather than the point is the claim. Finally the rule \emph{declines to judge} below four coarser groups holding two or more units
each. That floor is an \emph{attainability} precondition, not a power one: three groups of two
admit only $6!/(2!^{3}3!)=15$ distinct assignments, so the smallest reachable $p$ is
$1/15=0.067>\alpha$ and no design that small can reject at the nominal level at all, whereas four
groups of two admit $105$ and reach $0.0095$. The contract is therefore three-valued:
\textsc{halt}, the condition is violated; \textsc{indeterminate}, the design cannot support the
decision; and \textsc{pass}, which means \emph{not rejected}, not verified---at power $0.25$ for
$\rho=0.2$ three genuinely dependent designs in four also pass. Collapsing \textsc{indeterminate}
into \textsc{pass} is how an underpowered check comes to look like a satisfied one. No condition in
our own suite reaches the floor; the third-party artifact of \S\ref{sec:external} does, and the
rule abstained there rather than passing.

\emph{Canaries and seeds.} Six state channels are probed, separating whether a channel is
\emph{guarded} from whether the probe was \emph{effective}---an inert mutation reads as an
undetectable leak, which \rulename{L3.2} catches. Seeds live in three disjoint namespaces and one
whose outcomes have been inspected is burned for good; compared conditions draw one seed per
invariant cell, with pairing asserted on the realised arrays.

\section{Curated Regression Evaluation}
\label{sec:eval}

Supporting regression evidence, not the novelty result. Two minimal deterministic fixtures modelled
on the retrospective chains of \S\ref{sec:external} exercise a retrospective-label chain and a
learning-and-gating chain; neither carries a physical claim. The suite holds
\artv{fault_class_count}{\num{17}} \emph{curated} fault classes drawn from the defects that
motivated the rules, each injected in three environments differing in generator family, plus a clean
path per environment. Three denominators are easily conflated, so we fix them:
\artv{rule_count}{\num{19}} \emph{rules}, \num{17} fault \emph{classes}, \num{18}
\emph{conditions} per environment; rules and classes are not in bijection.

The chronological baseline is \emph{implemented and executed}, not reasoned about---three checks
asserting that fold extents are ordered and disjoint, that no row sits in a fold whose span
excludes it, and that no label used at a decision postdates it, run in the same sweep over the same
fixtures. Chronological splitting is a \emph{protocol property}, not a competing tool, so this
measures how much of the fault space it covers. It detects $\artv{chronological}{2/17}$
classes---invalid label-closure timing and a violated fold order, precisely the two that \emph{are}
ordering properties---and fires on no clean path; the contract detects
$\artv{contract}{17/17}$ identically in all three environments. We report \num{17} computations, not
$51$: the object handed to the detector is bit-identical across environments for fourteen classes,
so $\artv{injected_cells}{51/51}$ states \emph{determinism}. Clean paths were accepted with
identical verdicts, and \artv{red_fixture}{Sixteen} of nineteen rules are demonstrated red by a
fixture. The sweep runs in under \qty{2}{\second} on one laptop core, under
\qty{40}{\milli\second} per condition, \texttt{numpy} the only dependency---but that figure is the
\emph{checker}: \rulename{L1.1}, \rulename{L1.4} and \rulename{L4.6} each re-execute the pipeline
per variation, so their true cost is a multiple of pipeline runtime and is excluded.

For the one rule with a stochastic reference distribution the single clean run is not the
evidence---the measured $\artv{l47_rate}{0.031}$ false-halt rate is.

One clean-path false positive was not designed: the first run reported three, all
\rulename{L4.4}, one per environment---caused by the fixture, not the detector, since the

\section{Evidence From Outside This Project}
\label{sec:external}

Everything to this point was produced here; this section is not. Table~\ref{tab:rules} collects it.

\emph{The frozen rule on real catalogue records.} We applied \rulename{L4.7} unchanged---defaults,
no argument overridden---to Space-Track \textsc{gp} history for \artv{real_objects}{\num{11}}
objects in the only five-day window where all eleven have element sets:
\artv{real_elsets}{\num{109}} element sets, and \artv{real_passes}{\num{331}} visible passes over
a ground station at \ang{24.80}\,N, \ang{120.997}\,E, \qty{100}{\metre}, above a \ang{10} mask,
sampled at \qty{30}{\second} over \qty{24}{\hour} from each element set's epoch, \textsc{sgp4} for
geometry only. Protocol, observables and predictions were registered before each analysis existed.
Unit counts are \num{101} and \num{305}, not \num{109} and \num{331}: an element set republished
under a new catalogue identifier keeps its epoch, so eight records and the \num{26} passes they
generate collapse onto units already present.

\emph{The observable decides whether the rule can say anything.} Grouping element sets by object on
publication lag, and passes by element set on mean elevation, both passed at
$\hat{\rho}=0.000,\ p=1.000$---a ties signature, the estimator truncating at zero, so not an effect
size---against our predicted halts. Every object's lag sits at \qtyrange{6.3}{9.2}{\hour} here,
consecutive element sets are propagated a day apart so their passes overlap in geometry, and
elevation is a deterministic function of the grouping, so neither could speak to
\S\ref{sec:whysat}\,v, which is about along-track \emph{error}. One object halted, but across the
fourteen decisions those groupings make $P(\ge 1$ halt$)$ under the global null is $0.51$.

\emph{On the observable that claim is about, the rule halts.} Differencing each element set against
its successor---both propagated to a pass midpoint, in-track component, no model fitted---gives
\artv{at_passes}{\num{269}} passes, median $\lvert$in-track$\rvert$ \qty{0.22}{\kilo\metre} with a
tail to \qty{559}{\kilo\metre}. Grouped by generating element set, \rulename{L4.7} \textsc{halts} at
$\hat{\rho}=\artv{at_icc_d1}{0.498}$, one-sided $95\%$ upper bound $\artv{at_icc_d1_up}{0.636}$;
element sets within objects halt at $\artv{at_icc_d3}{0.284}$. The registered prediction held here,
and this is the one real-data result bearing on the premise: for an error-valued quantity the pass
is too fine a unit, which is what \S\ref{sec:whysat}\,v asserts. Reversing the declared coarser
level changes the statistic and not the decision, so the rule adjudicates whether the declared
level is adequate, never which level is right.

\emph{The unmodified contract on a third-party artifact.} We fixed inclusion criteria, selected one
public learning-assisted satellite artifact---a spacecraft-telemetry \textsc{lstm} from another
institution~\cite{hundman2018telemanom}---froze its commit, then ran the contract. No detector was
touched: its \texttt{sha256} is identical before and after and equal to the pre-registration hash.
All \artv{ext_classified}{\num{19}} rules are classified five ways, because on someone else's
artifact the outcome that matters is the one refusing to guess: \artv{ext_pass}{\num{5}}
\textsc{pass}, \artv{ext_halt}{\num{3}} \textsc{halt}, \artv{ext_indet}{\num{1}}
\textsc{indeterminate}, \artv{ext_na}{\num{5}} not applicable, \artv{ext_nobs}{\num{5}}
\emph{not observable}. Scoring those five as passes would make \num{5} of \num{9} decided rules
into \num{10} of \num{14}.

\rulename{L4.7} ran on the artifact's own per-channel errors and \emph{abstained} at the grouping
its authors declare---two spacecraft, below its floor of four---then decided and passed at a finer
grouping the artifact also exposes: the abstention semantics behaving as claimed on data nobody
here produced. One halt has methodological weight: overlapping windows sharing \num{259} of
\num{260} samples are shuffled and then split by fraction, so the early-stopping validation set is
drawn from the training window's own time span. We scope it to that signal, not the reported test
results, and the practice is common. The other two are provenance, and would fire on most research
repositories: a declared configuration value the implementation may silently reduce, and no hash
binding a run to its inputs. Where provenance is
incomplete we record that the artifact does not expose enough, never that its authors erred.

\emph{Does a detected violation matter?} We pre-registered one intervention, froze the detector's
hash, then corrected only the partition: carving the validation block chronologically and dropping
the \num{259} windows that make source-timestep overlap impossible takes overlap from
\qty{\artv{cons_overlap_pct}{100}}{\percent} of the validation support to zero and
\rulename{L4.1} from halt to pass. Over \artv{cons_seeds}{\num{5}} paired seeds it changed which
model early stopping selected \artv{cons_sel_changed}{\num{5}} times out of five, every checkpoint
differing. The reported detection metric moved on three seeds but \emph{in both directions},
median zero, so we report a selection consequence and not a stable downstream one---and this
channel could not show one, its target taking a single value in training and two in test. We claim
nothing about the upstream paper's validity or about improving anomaly detection.

Two retrospective case studies from the stopped programme are in the artifact: both predate the
contract, so no halt was ever run, and the fixtures of \S\ref{sec:eval} were modelled on them.

\section{Threats to Validity, Limitations and Conclusion}
\label{sec:threats}

\emph{The faults and the rules share an origin}: both were developed in one programme, mostly by
the same hand, so $17/17$ measures detector \emph{reachability}---a fixture written to trip rule
$X$ trips rule $X$---not mutation adequacy~\cite{jia2011mutation}, and hand-authored mutants are
known to overstate suite strength~\cite{just2014mutants}. Only one fault was specified before any detector existed, far too few to support a claim about
faults the suite lacks, so we make none. It establishes regression coverage of \emph{represented}
faults: these violations, once fixed, cannot silently return. The denominator is curated, not
sampled.

\emph{Injection is at contract-input level.} The fixtures hand every rule its own input attribute:
of the seventeen mutated objects only six reach more than one consumer---and in all six that
consumer is another check, never a computation producing a reported number---so for the other eleven a
condition shows a rule firing on a broken input rather than catching a defect in a working
pipeline. This is the sharpest limit on the evaluation. \rulename{L2.2}, \rulename{L2.3} and \rulename{L4.5} fire in no
\emph{curated} condition, which by our own standard is not evidence they can fail---though
\rulename{L4.5} did halt on the third-party artifact, which is the stronger demonstration.

\emph{Four rules are weaker than their statement.} \rulename{L4.6} draws its power from the
caller's variation set: it refutes a manifest omitting an input already suspected, while the input
nobody thought of is absent from the sweep by construction. \rulename{L4.3} is gated on a
self-reported flag, so a pipeline declaring itself aggregated silences it, and it keeps the fixed
threshold whose null size we measured at $0.17$. \rulename{L4.5} checks that a manifest and self-hash exist, not that
either is correct, and \rulename{L4.7} has power only against gross unit errors.

\emph{Three objects the contract names but does not check.} Covariate-coupled label availability,
the harder failure of \S\ref{sec:whysat}, has no rule and no fault: found by an \emph{ad hoc}
diagnostic, never promoted, so it has no identifier and no red fixture. $t_d$ is declared, not
checked: under periodic provisioning the defensible $t_d$ is the refresh instant, and declaring the
transmission instant lets every availability check pass while admitting a refresh interval of
unheld state. And the availability clock rests on an \emph{optimistic proxy}:
\texttt{CREATION\_DATE} is when a message was created, a lower bound on when it could be
retrieved---batch publication and reprocessing add unmeasured lag, and we found the same element
set republished under a new identifier with a later stamp---so \rulename{L1} has one-sided error,
able to pass a feature that was in fact unavailable but never to fail one that was available.
Epoch~$\le$~publication is likewise sometimes false, in \artv{timing_outlier_objects}{one} of
\artv{lag_objects}{\num{11}} objects over its full history and none in our window. We report that
per object, not pooled over \artv{lag_records}{\num{63727}} records, because the record is the
wrong unit here exactly as in the experiments the contract governs: the object-level median lag is
\qty{\artv{lag_object_median_h}{6.36}}{\hour} and positive for every object.

\emph{The axes of variation are narrow.} \S\ref{sec:external} is one external artifact and one
channel, not a survey. Nor is the contract complete: six protected objects and six state channels are operational
enumerations, not completeness claims. \rulename{L4.7} presumes a \emph{unique} next coarser level
and orbital data supplies none---element sets and deployment episodes cross-cut---and the level we
omit is the one most likely to matter: drag mismodelling driven by solar and geomagnetic activity is
common-mode across every object and element set in an interval, so a design exchangeable at the
element set can still fail at the space-weather level. Showing a rule catches defects nobody anticipated needs faults authored independently of the
detectors and injected at program level.

The lesson is not that a longer checklist replaces a temporal split, but that some validity
obligations are \emph{relational}: they concern how two executions differ, or whether the chosen
unit stays exchangeable one physical level coarser. Making those executable---and letting the gate
abstain when the design cannot support a decision---turns an assumption into a claim CI can
enforce. Satellite software is where we found them because it separates the clocks, the geometry
and the publisher a single-table view hides. We have shown the checks fire on the violations we
represented, cheaply enough for every commit; what remains is to run them against faults, and
pipelines, we did not write.
